# $100M Leads (Hormozi, 2023): notes for Money X-Ray

Page refs are PDF page numbers of the uploaded file (389 pp).

## 1. Core principles

**Engaged leads, not leads (p40-41).** A lead is anyone you can contact. What matters is an *engaged* lead: someone who shows interest (opts in, replies, follows). Advertising's real output is engaged leads.

**Lead magnets (p47-70).** A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem. Solving it should expose the next problem, and the paid offer solves that one. There are three types (p51-55): *reveal a problem* (a diagnosis), *sample/trial*, or *one step of a multi-step process*. Test the name, headline first (p57-62). Make it easy to use on a phone, in several formats (p63). Make it "darn good": "give away the secrets, sell the implementation." (p64-65). End with a CTA that says what to do and why now (p65-68).

**The core four (p76).** These are the only ways to let anyone know about anything:
- warm 1-to-1: warm outreach
- warm 1-to-many: posting content
- cold 1-to-1: cold outreach
- cold 1-to-many: paid ads

**Warm outreach (p82-106).** Start with everyone you have permission to contact. Reach out to 100 a day, personally, up to three touches each (p84-86). When people reply, use A-C-A: acknowledge, compliment, ask (p86-87). Don't ask them to buy. Ask "do you know anyone who...", which lets them self-select (p90-91). Make the first five free in exchange for using it, giving feedback and an honest review (p94-96). Once referrals start, begin charging and step prices up (p100). Keep the list warm, e.g. Dean Jackson's 9-word email: "Are you still looking to [desire]?" (p102). His benchmark is 100 reach-outs → ~20 replies → ~4 take the free offer → ~1 buyer (p104).

**Free content: hook, retain, reward (p113-129).** The compounding asset is the audience, not the individual posts (p111).
- **Hook:** pick a topic from far past, recent past, present, trending or manufactured experiences (p116-119). Headlines use two or more of recency, relevance, celebrity, proximity, conflict, unusual or ongoing (p120-121). Match the platform's native format (p122).
- **Retain:** create curiosity with lists, steps and stories (p123-125).
- **Reward:** deliver on the hook. Think value per second: "no such thing as too long, only too boring" (p126). Long-form is short content units chained together (p127-128).

Lessons: say "How I", not "How to"; remind more than you teach; win a puddle before the ocean (p143-144). 78% of his buyers had watched at least two long-form pieces before booking (p143).

**Give:ask ratio (p131-138).** TV runs about 3.5:1; growing platforms give far more. His preference is to "give until they ask": give in public, ask in private (p132). The moment you start asking is the moment growth slows (p133). There are two ways to ask:
- **Integrated:** a short ask inside the content, placed after a valuable moment or at the end (p134-135).
- **Intermittent:** for example, 10 give posts and then 1 ask post. This works best on short-form (p136).

Either ask points to the lead magnet or the core offer. If unsure, use the lead magnet because it's lower risk (p138). The thank-you page after the opt-in shows the paid offer (p136).

**Paid ads (p192-231).** Every ad is a callout plus value plus a CTA (p198-217). Value comes from the What-Who-When frame: the value equation and its opposites, seen through other people's eyes, across past, present and future (p205-211). He wants LTGP:CAC of at least 3:1 (p223), ideally with first-30-day profit covering acquisition cost (p225-228). Your best free content makes your best ads (p229). Do ads last (p231).

**More, Better, New (p236-247).**
- **More** first: crank volume.
- **Better:** find the step where most people drop off (the constraint, p240). Run one test per platform per week, log every result, and move on after four failed tries (p241-242).
- **New:** go in this order: new placement → new platform → new core-four activity (p246).

**Rule of 100 (p237-238).** Do 100 primary actions a day for 100 days. For content that means 100 minutes a day making it and at least one post a day. For outreach it means 100 reach-outs a day. "Open to goal" goes further: work until you hit the outcome, not a set number of hours (p366-367).

**Lead getters (p252-356).**
- **Referrals:** most businesses don't get them because the product isn't remarkable or they never ask (p266). Six goodwill builders (p268-277) include better-fit customers, *lower* expectations then over-deliver (p270-271) and fast wins in the first 48 hours (p275). Then ask, treating the ask as an offer (p279). Options: one- or two-sided rewards, ask at checkout, unlockable bonuses (p280-283).
- **Employees and agencies:** train with document, demonstrate, duplicate (p297-298); hire agencies to learn a skill, then bring it in-house (p311-313).
- **Affiliates:** six steps (p326): find businesses that already hold your leads, make an offer, qualify, pay off your max allowable CAC in tiers (p333-335), launch with whisper-tease-shout (p338-340), then integrate, ideally by having them give away your lead magnet (p341).

## 2. Applied to Money X-Ray

**Free lead magnet: "Subscription Finder"** (a reveal-the-problem type).
- **The narrow problem:** "What recurring charges am I paying?" The user pastes one short prompt and uploads one statement. The AI lists recurring charges, groups duplicates, flags price rises and unknown merchants, and totals them.
- **What it reveals:** this was one account and one kind of leak. The full picture is still unanswered: where the other $1,500 goes, the fees and interest, the forgotten card, the payoff plan. That is the paid product.
- **Ending:** the prompt ends with a plain-language note saying what it didn't check, plus the link.

Build notes:
- Deliver it as a free Stan Store product with email opt-in. The thank-you page and delivery email introduce Money X-Ray with the sample family report (p136).
- Make it phone-first: copy button, a 60-second screen-recording, and a text version (p63).
- Test names before launch with a community poll (p62), e.g. "Subscription Finder" vs "Forgotten Charge Finder".

**Content plan.** Use width-then-depth: one vertical clip goes to both Shorts and Reels (p140).

*Long-form (weekly)* is a manufactured or recent-past story (p117-119): "X-Raying the average American family", episode by episode.
Hook with the paradox ($1,500 left on paper, Visa growing); retain with findings in turn inside a story; reward with the real numbers and payoff comparison. Use "How I" framing; the founder's own redacted statements are the strongest "recent past" topic.

*Shorts/Reels (daily)* use one content unit per clip: one finding, one number, a clean ending.

**15 hooks** (all from the fictional demo family or general how-to; label the family as fictional on screen):
1. "This family has $1,500 left every month. So why is their Visa growing?"
2. "They thought they had 3 streaming services. The statement says 6."
3. "Netflix raised their price. Nobody in the house noticed."
4. "There's a $45 charge every month. Nobody knows what it is."
5. "This credit card costs them $122 a month just in interest."
6. "$340 in fees and interest in 3 months. Here's every line."
7. "Same debt, two plans: 62 months vs 23 months."
8. "They forgot they had a Discover card. It didn't forget them."
9. "Paste this into ChatGPT before you cancel anything."
10. "Why money piles up in checking while the card balance grows."
11. "Avalanche vs snowball with one family's real numbers."
12. "I made AI balance a statement to $0.00. Here's where it got stuck."
13. "If you take home $7,000 a month and still feel broke, watch this." (if-then callout, p200)
14. "3 charges to look for on your statement tonight."
15. "How I'd read a bank statement in 10 minutes."

**Where the CTA goes.**
- **Long-form:** use an integrated ask placed right after the biggest reward moment, usually the payoff comparison or "the $45 mystery" (p135). That's the 30-second promo slot; repeat in one line at the end, pinned comment and description.
- **Shorts/Reels:** use intermittent asks. Roughly 1 ask clip per 8-10 give clips (p136). Point to the free Subscription Finder, not the $39.99 product (p138). The bio link goes to Stan.
- **Email:** the lead-magnet list gets mostly value emails, with an occasional ask and the 9-word email: "Are you still trying to find where your money goes?" (p102).

**First 100 buyers.** Treat the audience as the warm list.
1. **First 5-10 free X-Rays** go to commenters or subscribers (p95). In exchange they give feedback, permission to share *redacted* findings, and an honest review. Redact names, account numbers and Zelle/Venmo counterparties first.
2. **One-to-one replies.** Reply to every comment and DM with A-C-A, and offer the free Subscription Finder (p86-87).
3. **Contacts.** Text and DM personal contacts with the "do you know anyone who..." framing (p90-91).
4. **Price steps.** A real launch price step-up is fine *only if it actually happens* (p100).
5. **Funnel math.** Rough assumption (mine, not the book's): if 5-10% of Subscription Finder users buy, 100 buyers needs about 1,000-2,000 opt-ins.
6. **Paid ads later.** At $39.99 one-time, a 3:1 LTGP:CAC ratio means paying about $13 or less per buyer (p223). Hard on cold traffic: later, run the clips that already convert (p229).

**Referrals and affiliates (later).**
- **Referrals:** offer a two-sided code ("give $10, get $10" or a bonus module, p280-283) shown on the report's last page. That's the moment of peak goodwill. Make the product remarkable first; lower expectations so it can over-deliver (p270-271).
- **Affiliates:** personal-finance and debt-free creators, budgeting coaches, money newsletters (p326-327) give away the Subscription Finder with their own link (p341); tiered commission from max allowable CAC (p333-335); FTC disclosure required.

**Weekly rule-of-100 schedule (solo creator, about 2-3 h/day).** Each weekday is 100 minutes making content plus about 100 one-to-one touches (comment and DM replies, emails to people who replied):

| Day | Content (100 min) | Touches / other |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Script the weekly long-form; review last week's test log; pick 1 test per platform (p241) | Replies |
| Tue | Film the long-form; cut 2 Shorts from it | Replies |
| Wed | Edit and publish the long-form (manual post, p145); post Short | Replies; email to list |
| Thu | Film 3-4 standalone Shorts | Replies |
| Fri | Post Short; plan next week's hooks | Replies; outreach to 5 possible affiliates (later) |
| Sat/Sun | Post 1 Short each (pre-filmed) | Light replies |

Hormozi says 100 days straight. Five strong days plus light weekends is sustainable solo. Track growth monthly (p146), opt-ins and buyers weekly.

## 3. Warnings: what not to copy for finance

- **Made-up urgency and scarcity.** He endorses "make up a reason" (p68), countdown discounts (p67), "only five spots" (p90) and price-ups every five (p100). A digital product has no natural scarcity. Use only real deadlines and real price changes. No resetting timers, no fake spots.
- **"Guaranteed, insanely fast, no effort" (p89-90)** and **ridiculous-result callouts** like "government handing out checks" (p200-201). Never promise savings, a debt-free date or accuracy. Hormozi himself says "without lying or exaggerating" (p90). Label the demo family fictional; guarantee a refund, not a result.
- **Nightmare and status copy (p206-209).** Spouse/kids/rivals shame angles are cruel to a stressed money audience. Lead with clarity and relief.
- **Cold outreach at volume (p166, p173).** Scraped lists, robo-dialers and email/SMS blasts mean spam and legal exposure (TCPA, CAN-SPAM). Never cold-DM strangers about their finances.
- **Expiring gift card "I don't want to waste it" (p284).** This is a manufactured pressure tactic. Skip it.
- **Paid entry for affiliates (p330-332).** Charging affiliates to join reads as MLM for a $40 product. Don't do it.
- **Ad targeting.** Lookalikes from buyer lists (p196-197) and yes-questions that assume someone's debt can break platform rules on personal or financial attributes and creep people out. Check each platform's financial-services ad policy.
- **Real data in content.** Every real statement shown on camera must be fully redacted, and viewers' data must only be used with consent.
