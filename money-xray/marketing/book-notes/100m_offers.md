# $100M Offers (Alex Hormozi, 2021): notes for Money X-Ray

Page numbers are PDF page numbers from the uploaded file (211 pages). Read in full.

## 1. Core principles

**What an offer is.** The offer is what you give, how they pay and the terms (p.30). A "Grand Slam Offer" can't be compared to anything else, so the buyer is choosing between you and nothing (p.45-46).

**The commodity trap.** If a buyer thinks "these are the same, I'll take the cheaper one," you've become a commodity and prices get pushed down to bare survival (p.44). Pricing by copying the average competitor means copying businesses that are broke (p.71). Being the second-cheapest gains you nothing (p.70). Money X-Ray's commodity risk is real: free prompts and $5-15 Etsy prompt packs look the same at a glance.

**Starving crowd.** Order of importance: market, then offer, then persuasion (p.59). A good market has four things: massive pain, purchasing power, easy to target and growing (p.55-57). Niching raises price for the same content ($19 generic course vs $1,000+ niche, p.62-63). "The pain is the pitch" (p.56).

**Charge what it's worth.** People buy when the value they get is clearly bigger than the price (p.69). Raise value first, then price (p.70). Higher prices make buyers more committed and more likely to use what they bought (p.73-76). A price far above the field signals a different category (wine study, p.75).

**Value equation** (p.86-99): value = (dream outcome x perceived likelihood of achievement) / (time delay x effort and sacrifice). Big claims about the dream outcome are easy and lazy. The edge comes from driving time and effort toward zero (p.87-88). What counts is the buyer's *perception*: London's countdown boards raised rider satisfaction more than faster trains did (p.88). Likelihood rises through proof, track record and guarantees (p.92-93). Give buyers fast early wins so they stay on to the long-term outcome (p.93-94). "Fast beats free": people will pay for speed in markets where free options exist (p.95).

**Problems and solutions** (p.112-117). (1) Name the dream outcome. Sell the vacation, not the plane ride (p.112). (2) List every obstacle in the order the buyer meets them, including what happens just before and just after using the product (p.113). Each obstacle falls into one of four buckets: "not worth it", "won't work for me", "too hard", "takes too long" (p.114). (3) Turn each obstacle around into a "how to..." solution (p.115-116). Solve *every* one, because a single unanswered objection can lose the sale. (eating-out guide story, p.128).

**Trim and stack** (p.121-134). Brainstorm delivery formats: 1:1, small group or one-to-many; DIY, done-with-you or done-for-you; and the 10x / 1/10th price test (p.127). Cut anything high-cost and low-value, then anything low-cost and low-value. Keep the high-value items (p.129). One-to-many assets cost a lot to build once and almost nothing after that, which is the best ratio (Excel meal-plan generator, p.129-131). Name each bundle and attach a value to it (p.131-133).

**Scarcity** (p.149-156) works on quantity. There are three types: limited slots, limited bonuses, and never available again (p.152). The most ethical version is honest capacity: publish your real limit (p.155).

**Urgency** (p.158-162) works on time. There are four types: rolling cohorts, seasonal promotions, price or bonus deadlines, and exploding opportunities. Deadlines have to be real, or you lose credibility (p.160). Announce price increases before they happen (p.161-162).

**Bonuses** (p.165-172). Add bonuses instead of discounting, because discounts teach buyers your price is negotiable (p.166). Each bonus should have a benefit-led name, answer one specific objection, carry proof and a stated, justified value (p.167-168). Tools beat trainings (p.168). Bonuses can solve the buyer's *next* problem (p.168).

**Guarantees** (p.175-189). Risk is the biggest objection (p.175). There are four types: unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee ("all sales final") and implied (performance pay) (p.176). Run the numbers: 30% more sales with refunds doubling from 5% to 10% still leaves 1.23x net (p.176). A guarantee needs a consequence: "if not X in Y, we do Z" (p.177). Broad unconditional guarantees suit low-ticket consumer products (p.182). A guarantee can't rescue a weak product (p.189).

**Naming: MAGIC** (p.192-201). The parts are **M**agnetic reason why, **A**vatar, **G**oal, **I**nterval and **C**ontainer word (System, Blueprint, Reset...). Use 3-5 of them. Hormozi warns that a numeric claim paired with a time frame reads as a guarantee and ad platforms reject it (p.196). When an offer gets stale, change the ad creative first, then the copy, then the name. Change the offer itself last (p.199).

## 2. Money X-Ray offer design

**Dream outcome (the vacation):** "Finally know where every dollar went, with a clear plan for the leaks and the card debt, without an app or a monthly fee." The status driver is relief and control, not showing off.

**Obstacles turned into solutions:**

| Obstacle (bucket) | Solution | Core or bonus |
|---|---|---|
| "I don't know how to get my statements" (effort) | Statement Finder: phone screenshots and steps for the 10-15 biggest US banks and card issuers, CSV vs PDF, how many months to pull | Core |
| "I'm scared to upload my bank data" (likelihood) | Privacy Setup: how to turn off training or use temporary chats in ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, what to black out, no bank linking. The prompt also tells the AI never to repeat full account numbers | Core |
| "AI makes mistakes" (likelihood) | Balance-checked to $0.00. The report lists what it couldn't read, and a "Check the Top 5" sheet has the buyer verify the biggest findings against the statements | Core (main proof point) |
| "No time" (time) | One paste, guided uploads, a measured real-world run time (measure it before quoting it) | Core |
| "I don't get finance" (effort) | Plain-English report, a one-page glossary and a "what this means for you" line under every section | Core |
| "I don't know what to cut" (dream) | Leak List ranked by monthly cost, plus Cancel and Call Scripts (cancel a subscription, ask for a fee reversal, ask for a lower APR). Say plainly that issuers often say no | Bonus |
| "Debt feels hopeless" (likelihood) | Avalanche vs snowball plan, month by month, with interest saved shown as an estimate | Core |
| "Free plan limits / file too big" (effort) | Free-Plan Mode: batching steps tested on each free tier, and what to do when an upload fails | Core |
| "I'll slip back next month" (next problem, p.168) | Monthly 15-minute Re-Check prompt | Bonus |
| "My partner and I fight about money" (next problem) | Money Date conversation guide for going through the report together | Bonus |
| "I want to see it, not read it" | Notion dashboard | Bonus |

Keep the budget with cheaper local alternatives out of the stack until it ships. If it's announced, call it a free update only if you're committed to delivering it.

**Value stack with honest anchors.** Hormozi values his own bundles by the money they'll save (p.132). Don't do that here, because it reads as a savings promise. Anchor only to things a buyer can check: replacement cost and time. Re-check every price before publishing.
- Audit and debt plan: about a year of a bank-linking budgeting app (~$100/yr, verify) or an hour with a fee-only planner ($150-300+, verify)
- Statement Finder, Privacy Setup, Free-Plan Mode: put these in time terms ("saves the hour of hunting through bank menus"), not invented dollar figures
- Cancel and Call Scripts, Re-Check prompt, Money Date guide, Notion dashboard: modest stated values ($15-30 each), each with a one-line reason
- The total should land around $250-350 in honest comparables against the price. That gap is big enough without inflating anything.

**Price: $39 one-time ($39.99 is fine too).** Reasoning:
- It sits well above prompt packs, which signals a different category (p.75). It's below a year of any app, and "no subscription" is a real point of difference.
- It's less than one month of the demo family's card interest (~$122). Say that only as "for the example family."
- Hormozi's "make it sting" advice (p.76) is the wrong call for buyers who already feel broke.
- Plan an honest increase to $49 once there are real customer results, and announce it in advance (p.161-162).
- Never show a "regular price $97" that was never actually charged.

**Guarantee: the "Nothing New, Money Back" guarantee.** "Run the X-Ray on your statements. If it doesn't show you at least one thing about your money you didn't already know, email us within 30 days for a full refund. No forms." It's satisfaction-based and promises no savings. An unconditional guarantee is the right type for a low-ticket consumer product (p.182), and the refund math works at $39 (p.176). Add a support line on top: "If the balance check won't reach $0.00, send us the error and we'll help you fix it." Track refund rate. If abuse appears, move to a conditional guarantee ("ran it on at least one full month"). Don't switch to "all sales final":

**5 MAGIC names** (claims kept non-numeric, per p.196):
1. **Money X-Ray: The No-App Money Leak Finder** (Magnet: no app / Goal: leaks / Container: Finder)
2. **Money X-Ray: Where'd-It-Go Family Audit** (Avatar: families / Goal: answers / Container: Audit)
3. **Money X-Ray: One-Evening Leak & Debt Game Plan** (Interval: one evening, only if testing supports it / Container: Game Plan)
4. **Money X-Ray for Decent-Paycheck, Still-Broke Households** (Avatar-led, built for Shorts hooks)
5. **New Year Money X-Ray Reset** (seasonal Magnet / Container: Reset. Swap the season to Tax-Season or Back-to-School, p.160-161)

Keep "Money X-Ray" fixed; rotate the wrapper (p.199).

**Honest urgency and scarcity.** A digital file can't run out, so no "only 50 left."
- Launch price with a real end date and a real increase afterwards (p.161-162).
- Seasonal wrappers with real start and end dates: New Year, tax season, back to school (p.160).
- Bonus urgency: a monthly live "X-Ray Night" Q&A, or its replay, for people who buy during that month's window (p.168-169). Real capacity limits on a live Zoom are honest scarcity (p.155).
- True cost-of-delay: card interest builds every month. Show it with the demo's labeled numbers or the buyer's own, never as a scare figure.

## 3. Warnings: where the book crosses the line for a finance product

- **Made-up scarcity.** "Only you get to draw where that line is 'full'" (p.156), the scripted "a client dropped out, so a spot opened" (p.159), and "just make one up" for a reason why (p.187). Don't use any of them. No countdown timers that reset.
- **Valuing bonuses by the savings they'll produce** (p.132), and making bonuses "eclipse" the core through a bias Hormozi himself says isn't logical (p.168). For money products this turns into implied promises and fictitious price comparisons (FTC territory).
- **Exploding opportunity** (p.162) uses crypto and arbitrage examples. "Act now or lose thousands" is hype, so use the buyer's own interest figures instead.
- **Outsized and conditional guarantees** ("find $X or double your money back", p.182) would be savings guarantees. Don't offer them. Implied or performance pricing like "% of what we save you" is also out.
- **Time and number claims in names** (p.196). "Debt-free in 23 months" came from a fictional test family. Always label it as an example, never as a typical result.
- **Status and shame framing** (p.91-92). Don't write "your friends will see...". Frame benefits as relief and control.
- **Proof.** His proof is survey averages from his own clients (p.77-78). Money X-Ray needs real, typical, permissioned results with personal details redacted. Never claim "100% accurate". The visible $0.00 balance check is what raises perceived likelihood.
- **Partner and affiliate bonuses** (p.169-171). Commission deals on cards, loans or balance transfers are a conflict of interest in a debt product. Avoid them, or disclose them clearly.
- **"Charge so it stings"** (p.76) and "egregious" pricing (p.84) don't fit an audience whose problem is feeling broke.
