# Money X-Ray copy and offer playbook

Built from the 10 books the founder supplied, read in full by 8 reader agents. Their notes, with page references, are in `book-notes/`. This page is the decision layer: what we say, how we sell, and what we never do.

Source caveats:
- "Ogilvy on Advertising" and "Great Leads" are Bookey summaries, not the books. Check the originals before quoting either author publicly.
- "Principles of Physiological Psychology" is Volume I of Wundt (1904), mostly anatomy.
- "The 16-Word Sales Letter" uses ten questions, not sixteen.

## 1. Who we're talking to (Schwartz)

- **Warm YouTube viewers** are problem-aware or desire-aware when the promo plays. The story just made the problem vivid. Lead with the desire, then the mechanism, then the product. Don't open on price.
- **Cold Shorts and Reels viewers** are problem-aware at best, and many won't admit they're "bad with money." Open with identification (good income, still broke), never with the product or the word "budget."
- **Store page visitors** know the product exists. They need the mechanism, the demo, the offer.
- **The market is crowded.** Everyone has heard "track your spending" and "cancel subscriptions," and bigger savings claims are both dead and dishonest. We win with a new mechanism shown plainly, plus identification for cold traffic.

## 2. The core idea

| Piece | Our version |
|---|---|
| Golden thread / One Belief (Masterson, Albuquerque) | "You're not bad with money. You've just never seen it all in one place." |
| Mechanism (Schwartz) | **The $0.00 Check**: the audit isn't finished until every dollar in your statements is accounted for and every account balances. Whatever's left gets named. |
| Preemptive claim (Hopkins) | "Every account is balance-checked to $0.00 before you see a single finding." Careful audits do this; nobody says it. Never word it as "zero errors." |
| Enemy (Albuquerque) | Billing practices, not people: set-and-forget subscriptions, quiet price rises, fees, 23% card interest, and apps that charge monthly to show you your own money. |
| Main desire (Whitman's Life-Force 8) | Freedom from fear: the dread of checking the balance. Supporting desires: care for family, comfortable living, beating the card company. |
| Promise | To show, never to save. "You'll see your numbers clearly." |

## 3. The offer (Hormozi, $100M Offers)

**Dream outcome:** know where every dollar went, with a plan for the leaks and the card debt, without an app or a monthly fee.

**Every obstacle, solved:**

| Obstacle | Solution | In product |
|---|---|---|
| "Where do I get statements?" | Statement finder: app, email, bank website; CSV vs PDF | Built into the prompt; add a one-page guide |
| "I'm scared to upload bank data" | No bank login; CSV has no account numbers; the AI never repeats personal details; privacy settings guide for each AI | Prompt done; guide to write |
| "AI makes mistakes" | The $0.00 Check, plus "check your top 5 findings" | Built in |
| "I don't get finance" | Plain English, terms explained | Built in |
| "Free plan limits" | One account at a time, checkpoints, paste fallback | Built in, tested |
| "Debt feels hopeless" | Avalanche vs snowball plan | Built in, tested |
| "I don't know what to cut" | Ranked leak list; cancel and call scripts (honest: issuers often say no) | Bonus to write |
| "Cheaper options near me" | Budget part with local alternatives | Not built; don't list until it ships |
| "I'll slip back" | Monthly checkup | Built in |
| "My partner and I argue about money" | Money date guide for reading the report together | Bonus to write |
| "I want to see it" | Notion dashboard | To build |

**Price:** launch at **$39** one time (Hormozi; $39.99 is fine too, per Whitman's "99 reads as value"). Announce in advance a real move to $49 once real customer results exist, and keep that date. Don't "make it sting": the buyer already feels broke.

**Value anchors:** only things a buyer can check, like a year of a bank-linking budgeting app or an hour with a fee-only planner. Re-check the prices before publishing. Never a made-up "$497 value," never value measured in savings.

**Guarantee: "Nothing New, Money Back."** "Run Money X-Ray on your own statements. If it doesn't show you at least one thing about your money you didn't know, email us within 30 days for a full refund. No forms." Put it in a bordered box with the founder's signature, next to the button. It promises clarity, never savings. Track the refund rate.

**Name:** keep "Money X-Ray" fixed and test a wrapper: "Money X-Ray: The No-App Money Leak Finder," or seasonal: "New Year Money X-Ray Reset," "Tax-Season Money X-Ray."

**Honest urgency only:** a real launch price with a real end date; seasonal wrappers with real dates; a live monthly "X-Ray Night" Q&A, which is a real capacity limit; and the true cost of waiting, shown with the buyer's own interest figure. No countdown timers, no "only 50 left."

## 4. The free lead magnet (Hormozi, $100M Leads; Hopkins on samples)

**"Subscription & Fee Finder"**, a free Stan Store product, given for an email address:
- Paste one short prompt and upload one statement.
- It lists recurring charges, price rises, fees and unknown merchants, with a total.
- It ends by saying plainly what it didn't check: the other accounts, the $0.00 Check, interest, the debt plan. Then it links to the full product.
- The thank-you page and first email show the sample family's report.
- Test the name first ("Subscription Finder" vs "Forgotten Charge Finder").

## 5. Store page, phone first (Ogilvy, Whitman, Wundt)

One idea per phone screen. Picture, then headline, then copy. Dark text on white, sans serif at 16px or larger, no all-caps paragraphs, one accent color for the one number or button that matters.

1. **Screen 1:** a sample-report screenshot showing the contrast ("about $1,400 a month left over" next to "Visa still growing, $122 a month interest"), the headline, a one-line subhead, the button with the price.
2. **Screen 2:** the problem in the reader's words (identification).
3. **Screen 3:** how it works in 3 steps, with The $0.00 Check named.
4. **Screen 4:** the demo, with 5 captioned screenshots, each captioned "Sample data: fictional family."
5. **Screen 5:** reasons why it works (Hopkins), stated as specific facts.
6. **Screen 6:** what you get.
7. **Screen 7:** what it won't do: no advice, no promised savings, no bank login. Saying who it's not for builds trust.
8. **Screen 8:** privacy.
9. **Screen 9:** the guarantee box and the price.
10. **Screen 10:** FAQ, then the button again.

**Opening (Schwartz's gradualization, then Albuquerque's ten questions):**

> If your household brings home good money and still feels tight by the end of the month, this is for you.
> You've looked at your bank app. The big bills don't explain it.
> What usually explains it are the small things you never see all at once: a streaming price that went up, an app you forgot, a card that charges interest every month, a fee you didn't notice.
> Money X-Ray puts them in one place. Paste one prompt into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini (free plans work), upload your statements, and it runs The $0.00 Check: it doesn't finish until every dollar is accounted for. Whatever's left gets named.

The full draft sales letter is in `book-notes/16words_greatleads.md`. Update its numbers from the demo facts table before using it.

## 7. Video

**30-second mid-video promo** (Schwartz, Ogilvy): one desire, one mechanism.

| Seconds | Shows | Says |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | Bridge from the story; "Money X-Ray" said by second 5 | "That's what happens when good money disappears without a list. I built Money X-Ray for exactly this." |
| 5-15 | Screen demo: upload, then "$0.00 difference," then the findings (sample family label on screen) | "It reads every line of your statements, checks every account to the penny, then shows you what's leaking." |
| 15-22 | "No app. No bank login. Free plan works." | "It runs on the AI you already have. No app, no bank login." |
| 22-27 | Product shot, one-time price | "One payment, yours to keep. Educational, not financial advice." |
| 27-30 | Link | "Link in the description and the pinned comment. Now, back to the story." |

**Shorts and Reels** (Hormozi: hook, retain, reward):
- One finding per clip, one number on screen, end on the product name.
- Roughly one ask for every 8-10 clips that only give.
- The ask points to the free Subscription & Fee Finder, not the paid product.

Hooks (all from the labelled sample family):
1. "They thought they had 3 streaming services. The statement says 6."
2. "Netflix raised their price. Nobody in the house noticed."
3. "There's a $45 charge every month. Nobody knows what it is."
4. "This credit card costs them $122 a month just in interest."
5. "$523 in fees and interest in 3 months. Here's every line."
6. "Same debt, two plans: 62 months vs 23 months."
7. "They forgot they had a Discover card. It didn't forget them."
8. "Paste this into ChatGPT before you cancel anything."
9. "If you take home $7,000 a month and still feel broke, watch this."
10. "3 charges to look for on your statement tonight."

**Long-form series:** "X-Raying the average American family," then the founder's own redacted statements ("How I…" beats "How to…").

**Weekly rhythm** (Hormozi's rule of 100, scaled for one person): about 100 minutes of content a day, and reply to every comment and DM. One long-form a week, and a Short every day.

## 8. Numbers on screen (Wundt, Whitman)

- Show money as monthly and yearly together: "$122 a month, which is $1,464 a year." Monthly fits a budget decision; yearly sits next to the price ($39 vs $1,464).
- Group small leaks so they register: "6 subscriptions = $X a month."
- Give each finding a reference point: "Netflix $15.49 → $17.99 (+16%)."
- Concrete words over abstract ones: "the $45 charge you don't recognize," never "financial optimization."
- In the report the AI writes, one headline finding at the top, one accent color for money leaving, a calm tone.

## 9. Testing and tracking (Hopkins, Kennedy, Ogilvy)

- **Track every source separately:** a separate Stan link or product per channel (long-form, Shorts, Reels, email, bio), plus UTM tags per video. Check whether Stan's analytics reads UTMs; if not, use separate links and a checkout question, "Which video sent you?"
- **Judge on sales per 1,000 views and per visit,** not views or clicks.
- **Test one thing at a time,** in this order:
  1. Hook
  2. Store headline
  3. Free sample vs straight sale
  4. Which sample
  5. Short vs long page
  6. Price
- **Swap the two versions** between periods before calling a winner. A handful of sales decides nothing.
- **Ask buyers what their report found.** With permission and redaction, those findings become real testimonials.

## 10. Never (the line every book crossed somewhere)

- No fake scarcity, resetting countdowns, "only N left," or invented reasons for a deadline.
- No savings guarantees, no promised debt-free dates, no "100% accurate."
- No made-up reference prices or value stacks.
- No ads dressed as news, bank letters or government notices. Label paid and self-promotional content.
- No scaring people about debt, and no shaming. Every scary number sits next to the fix.
- No invented testimonials or authority. Real buyers only, with consent and redaction.
- No cold-messaging strangers about their finances, no scraped lists.
- No affiliate deals for cards, loans or balance transfers inside a debt product without clear disclosure, and preferably none.
- No hiding limits: state what free plans can't do, that PDFs are less tested, that AI can err, and that uploads go to the buyer's own AI provider.
- Redact all personal details from the founder's real statements in anything published.

## 11. Decisions for the founder

1. Price: $39 or $39.99 at launch, and the date it moves to $49.
2. Guarantee: adopt "Nothing New, Money Back," 30 days?
3. Which bonuses to write before launch (cancel and call scripts, money date guide, privacy guide, Notion dashboard).
4. Whether the budget part ships at launch or later.
5. The founder's true "I've been in your shoes" story, 3-4 sentences, for the store page.
