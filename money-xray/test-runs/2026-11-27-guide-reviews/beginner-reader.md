# Money X-Ray, a cold read by a first-time buyer

Who I am: 38, decent salary, broke by the 25th every month, not a finance person. I use the free ChatGPT plan on my iPhone. I paid $39 after a YouTube video, and I'm nervous about uploading bank statements. I read all 49 pages, screenshots included, on my phone and then on a laptop.

---

## 1. First impression (cover + pages 2-4)

**What I think I bought:** a prompt I paste into ChatGPT, plus a guide that tells me how to feed it my statements and what to do with the answer. Page 4 makes that clear in its first paragraph: "Money X-Ray turns ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini into a careful money investigator." Good. I understood the product in about 10 seconds.

**Cover (p1):** It looks premium and calm, more like a nice planner than a scammy course. The subtitle "The No-App Money Audit" made me stop for a second, though: the whole thing runs inside the ChatGPT *app*. I get what you mean (no budgeting app), but a skeptical buyer will notice. Something like "No budgeting app. No bank login." would be more accurate.

**Contents (p2):** The script font in "The Contents" is pretty but hard to read on a phone. The list itself is clear. Twenty-one chapters plus an appendix felt like a lot when I just want to know where my money goes.

**Note from Randy (p3):** This is what made me trust it. "No app to install. No bank login. No monthly fee." and "I ran it on my own statements first" are exactly what a nervous person wants to hear. The bottom 45% of the page is blank, though.

**Start Here (p4):** Strong page. "You're not bad with money. You've just never seen all of it in one place." landed with me, because that is literally my problem. "The fastest path" is the most useful paragraph in the guide. Two problems:
- "a save block you'll use again next month": I have no idea what a save block is yet.
- The fastest path ("read chapter 4 ... then chapter 6") **skips chapter 5, Your Privacy**. That's the one chapter I, the nervous person, need before uploading anything.

**Trust:** Medium-high after page 4. It went down later (see the p10 placeholder and the paid-plan screenshots).

## 2. Page by page

**p5, p12, p22, p32: Part dividers.** Four full black pages with a giant grey number. They look nice once. By the third one they felt like padding in a 49-page PDF, and they'd waste a lot of ink if I printed the worksheets.

**p6-7, How it works.**
- "The $0.00 Check" is a good idea and well explained. "Every account is balance-checked to $0.00 before you see a single finding" made me feel this isn't just ChatGPT guessing.
- "What it never counts twice" is great, and I'd never have thought about card payments double counting.
- p7 holds one leftover table row ("Your net worth") and one short paragraph, then about 75% blank. The table should not split for one row.

**p8-9, What you need.**
- "Paid plans are faster and can run the math in code." I don't know what "run the math in code" means, or whether my free plan is worse at math. This made me nervous.
- "The PDF and text file that came with this guide." What PDF? Is the prompt a *separate* PDF from this guide? Where's the text file? I downloaded one file from the checkout page. Name the files exactly ("Money_XRay_Prompt.txt") and say where they are.
- "3 months for every checking account, savings account and credit card." For me that's checking, savings, two cards and Venmo: **15 files**. That's when I realized "about 20 minutes" isn't my evening.
- Free plan row: "Daily limits; you can paste the CSV text instead." How do I paste CSV text on an iPhone? I wouldn't know how to open a CSV on my phone, let alone copy the rows.
- "CSV" appears here for the first time with no explanation. The glossary is on p43.
- The checklist breaks across pages: two boxes on p8, a stray line at the bottom, then two boxes on p9, and p9 is 90% empty.

**p10, Getting your statements.**
- **"[Screenshots coming: finding statements in a banking app, and downloading a CSV on the bank's website]"** I paid $39 for a finished product and this is a placeholder note left in. This one line hurt my trust more than anything else in the guide. It's also exactly the step I most need help with.
- "Your bank's website ... a 'Download' or 'Export' button on the transactions page that gives CSV files." On my phone? Most bank apps don't have that. Do I need a computer? If so, say it up front, because I don't use mine much.
- "Name them clearly ... 'Chase checking Jan'". How do I rename a file on an iPhone? Most people don't know you long-press in the Files app.
- "CSV (best)" vs "PDF (fine)" is clear. Good.

**p11, Your privacy.** This is the page I cared about most, and it's honest. "Covering details and choosing privacy settings lowers the risk. It doesn't remove it." I respect that. But:
- "Look in the app's settings under privacy or data controls ... check them yourself rather than relying on a screenshot." For a nervous non-techie this reads like a cop-out. Give me the exact taps for ChatGPT on iPhone (Settings > Data Controls > "Improve the model for everyone" off), and mention Temporary Chat if it fits. Then add a "names may change" line.
- "Covering your name and address is optional." How would I even cover it on a PDF on my phone? Markup? Tell me, or tell me not to bother.
- "Money X-Ray never sees them." Good. Say it in bold or a callout. It's the most reassuring sentence in the book and it's buried in the intro paragraph.

**p13, Run it step by step.**
- "The screenshots in Part 2 come from a real run on ChatGPT's paid plan." I'm on free, so every screenshot after this I read as "mine won't look like that."
- The left screenshot is an empty black chat. It adds nothing.
- The keyboard in the screenshots is Spanish ("Ñ", "Si" suggestions). It made me wonder whether this was a stock image. If it's Randy's real phone, fine, but it looks odd to me.
- The attached file is called "Money XRay Prompt(3)". The "(3)" looks sloppy, like the third download.
- "Paste the whole prompt, or attach the prompt PDF with the + button." Again, which file? Paste from where?
- "send it with a short message such as 'Start'", while the next screenshot (p14) shows the user typing "Tell me what do you need." The text and the screenshot don't match, and the screenshot has a grammar error.
- Step 2's screenshots are on the next page, and there's a big empty gap at the bottom of p13.

**p14.** The screenshots are tiny and you can't read them on a phone. The scroll-down arrow and the input bar cover the text ("Accepted file forma[arrow]"), and the right image is cut off at the top ("savings opportunities and n... ...s an educational"). Also: "No questionnaire. One short message" is nice. The bottom 40% of the page is empty.

**p15.**
- The file picker screenshot shows Randy's own clutter: "Money_XRay_Produ...eprint", "Complete_Ma ster_...eport". It looks like his work folder, not a demo.
- The right screenshot shows "Loan 3", "Loan 2" files and "9 statements received". That's fine, but it doesn't show what to do when ChatGPT free says I've hit my upload limit, which is what will actually happen to me.
- Step 4, "Let it work": how long? 30 seconds? 10 minutes? What does the screen look like while it's working? Can I lock my phone?
- "You shouldn't have to push it" is good reassurance.

**p16, Your report.**
- The section table is clear.
- The left screenshot is titled "2. Transaction reconciliation". I don't know the word "reconciliation", and it isn't in the glossary. The table said section 2 would be called "How much to trust this", so it doesn't match. The table is also cut off on the right ("March differen...").
- **Every number is blacked out**, so I never see what a $0.00 Check actually looks like, which is the whole trust mechanism. Use the fictional family's numbers in a screenshot instead.
- The donut chart's categories are "Restaurants", "Dunkin" and "Groceries". Is Dunkin a category now? It's funny, but it made me wonder whether the categories are random.
- **Clipped text at the very bottom of the page**: "Th $0.00 Ch... wh..." captions cut in half under the screenshots. It's a visible layout bug.

**p17.**
- "Reading 'left over'" might be the single most useful paragraph for me: "Many people find they have money left over on paper and still feel broke." That's me. It should be on page 4.
- The flags table is clear and practical.

**p18, Answering its questions.**
- "There are never more than 8 questions", yet the screenshot is headed "10. Information requiring clarification". After two mismatches, I started doubting the screenshots match the product.
- "SQ *JMK LLC": I don't know what "SQ *" means (it's Square). One line would help.
- "Reply 'ok' to keep all its guesses" is a great, low-effort escape hatch.

**p19.** One paragraph about cash, then 85% blank. "I mow lawns for about $80 a week, cash" is an oddly specific example. "Cash tips" or "Cash from selling stuff on Marketplace" would be more relatable.

**p20, On a free plan.** This is MY chapter, and it's half a page.
- "Open the CSV, copy the rows, paste them in. Long file? One month at a time." On an iPhone, how?
- "The chat hits its limit: Open a new chat, paste the prompt and your save block." What does hitting the limit look like on free ChatGPT? A banner? A model downgrade? I'd have no idea it happened.
- "Tested by hand ... In testing on a fictional family's statements, the free-plan path ... balanced every account." So it was never tested on *actual* free ChatGPT on a phone? Chapter 18 confirms the free test was "free-plan style". That worried me.
- Chapter 10 starts at the bottom of the page and the save block explanation spills onto p21.

**p21, Save block.**
- Half the page is a text column, the other half a screenshot that's mostly black. It starts mid-word ("nth."), and the rest of the page is blank.
- The screenshot shows "Recovery plan: 30, 60 and 90 days" and "gig income tax treatment". Neither is mentioned anywhere in the guide. It also doesn't match the save block format in the appendix (p49 is structured lines like "SAVE | Money X-Ray | ..."; the screenshot is prose). I can't tell what a correct one looks like.
- "Copy it into your notes app" is clear and simple. Good.

**p23-24, Subscriptions.**
- The three-question test is good, and "$15.99 a month is $191.88 a year" works on me.
- "If not, cancel it. You can always come back." That's slightly preachy but fair.
- "Pick your cuts from the menu ('Cut costs')": what menu? The guide never showed me a menu. It's only explained inside the appendix prompt (Step 5). I'd need a screenshot of that menu on p15 or p17.
- The cancel and dispute message templates are genuinely useful. This is where it felt worth money.
- p24's bottom half is empty.

**p25, Fees.** Good, practical table and scripts. "Fees and interest buy you nothing" is a nice line.

**p26-28, Debt plan.**
- Avalanche and snowball are explained clearly, finally in a way I get.
- "Cards with more than one rate": dense for me. "In the US, payments above the minimum usually go to the card's highest-rate balance first." I read it twice and still don't know what I'm supposed to do with it.
- p27 screenshots: "blended estimated rates", "Model assumptions", "lump-sum payment", "accelerated payment". Finance-speak, with the numbers blacked out again.
- The screenshot says Avalanche 26 months / Snowball 27 months, and the table right below says 23 / 23. Different data sets, but nothing says so. I thought one of them was wrong.
- The worked example table is great. "Minimums only: 62 months, $7,503" is the scariest and most motivating number in the guide.
- p28's bottom 60% is blank.

**p29, Net worth.** "Worth over $160,000 on paper, with almost nothing free once the cards are paid. That gap is why a household earning $7,290 a month can still feel broke." This hit home. Again: it belongs near the front.

**p30-31, Budget.**
- "a bit below your average, not a dramatic cut" is good, non-judgy advice.
- The "Instead of / Try" table has one row on p30 and the rest on p31. p31 is two-thirds empty.

**p33-34, Monthly checkup + troubleshooting.**
- "Same day every month, right after your statement closes." My cards and checking all close on different days. Which one?
- The troubleshooting table is useful, but it's missing the problems I'd actually hit on free ChatGPT: "You've reached your file upload limit", "ChatGPT says it can't read this file", ChatGPT refusing or saying it can't give financial advice, and "it made up a number".
- The table splits; p34 is 70% empty.

**p35, How it was tested.**
- "tested on a fictional household built from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics' 2025 spending averages" is credible. But "The automated tests were run by AI agents that never saw the answers" means nothing to a normal person, and honestly sounds a little weird. Say "We gave the prompt to fresh AI chats that didn't know the right answers, and checked their work."
- "Real statements, ChatGPT and Claude: Run by the founder." So one real person, on a paid plan. Be upfront that the free iPhone path hasn't been run end to end by a real customer, or go do it.
- "What it can't promise" is honest and well placed.

**p36-38, Sample report.** **This is the best part of the guide.** Real-looking numbers, a $0.00 table, a subscription list with flags. It's what made me *get it*. It's buried on page 36. I'd want it on page 5, or at least a one-page excerpt there.
- "Not uploaded: a Discover card." But section 6 later lists "Discover (27.24%)". Where did the rate come from, if it wasn't uploaded? Presumably they answered a question; say so.
- p38: "$270 short of their $300 goal". What goal? That's the first time "goal" appears in the sample. Then the page is 90% empty.

**p39-42, Worksheets.** I'm on an iPhone. I can't type into a table inside a PDF, and I'm not printing 4 pages. The debt list has a header and one row on p39, then continues on p40, which is 80% empty. p42 is 65% empty. Give me a Google Sheet / Numbers file or an Apple Notes template.

**p43, Glossary.** Good, plain definitions. It's missing: reconciliation, code / "no-code mode", "SQ *", Zelle, Notion, lump sum, liquid.

**p44, Checklist.** Excellent, and it's the whole guide on one page. It should be page 5, not page 44.

**p45-49, Appendix: the full prompt.**
- It's a wall of tiny monospace text, unreadable on a phone. Fine as a reference, but if the text file is missing, copying 5 PDF pages on an iPhone would pick up the page footers ("MONEY X-RAY 46 THE COMPLETE GUIDE") and break the prompt.
- p47 shows "Groceries ████████ $572". It looks like a redaction bar, so on first glance I thought something was censored.
- p47 and p48: "**Notion export:** the transactions as CSV for the Money X-Ray dashboard." **What dashboard?** It's never mentioned in the guide. It made me feel I'd bought half a product and there's an upsell coming.
- p48: the CSV header line runs off the right edge of the box ("...recurring,veri"). Clipped text.
- The prompt is full of instructions to the AI ("Don't lecture", "no 'Great question'"). Reading it actually raised my trust, because I could see it's careful. That's a plus.

## 3. Could I do the whole thing tonight from this guide alone?

**Honestly: maybe the first half, probably not the whole thing, and not in 20 minutes.** Here is every point where I'd get stuck:

1. **Finding the prompt file (p8, p13).** "The PDF and text file that came with this guide" / "attach the prompt PDF". I'd have to dig through my email or downloads to find out whether I even have them. *Need:* the exact file names and "it's in the same download as this guide / here's the link".
2. **Downloading statements on an iPhone (p10).** The screenshots are literally "coming". My bank's app shows PDF statements one at a time. CSV export I can't find on mobile. *Need:* real screenshots, an honest "CSV usually needs a computer; PDFs from the app are fine", and step-by-step help for the 5 biggest US banks and cards.
3. **Saving 15 PDFs into Files and renaming them (p10).** *Need:* "Tap Share > Save to Files", and long-press to rename.
4. **Privacy settings in ChatGPT (p11).** I'd stop here, nervous, unsure what to turn off. *Need:* exact taps for ChatGPT on iPhone.
5. **Free-plan upload limits (p8, p20).** Free ChatGPT will cap my uploads long before 15 files. The guide's fix, "Open the CSV, copy the rows", isn't something I can do on an iPhone with PDFs. *Need:* a realistic free-plan path: "Do your main checking + your main card tonight (6 files), the rest tomorrow", plus what the limit message looks like and what happens to the chat when it hits.
6. **Knowing it's working (p15 Step 4).** No time estimate, no picture of a free-plan "Next: Visa and Amex. Reply go" message. *Need:* one free-plan screenshot of that exact message.
7. **Reading the $0.00 Check (p16).** Every number is blacked out, so I don't know what "good" looks like. *Need:* a screenshot with fictional, readable numbers.
8. **The "menu" / "Cut costs" (p23).** Never shown. *Need:* a screenshot of the Step 5 menu.
9. **Hitting the chat limit (p20).** I wouldn't recognize it. *Need:* a description of what it looks like.
10. **Worksheets (p39-42).** Can't fill them in on a phone. *Need:* an editable version.
11. **"Dashboard" / Notion export (p47).** I'd pick option 4 from the menu out of curiosity and have nowhere to put the CSV. *Need:* either the dashboard, or remove the option.

Realistic estimate for me: 30-45 minutes to get statements onto my phone, a free-plan upload wall partway through, and finishing tomorrow from the save block, if I figured out the save block.

## 4. Was it worth $39?

**Mostly yes, if it works on my free plan. I'm not yet sure it will.** The prompt itself is clearly thought through (the appendix convinced me more than the marketing did). The sample report, the debt example, and the cancel, dispute and fee-refund scripts are worth real money to someone like me. The "liquid net worth" idea alone explained my "broke on paper" feeling.

What makes it feel like *less* than $39:
- The "[Screenshots coming...]" placeholder (p10). It feels unfinished.
- Every screenshot is from a paid plan, with every number blacked out.
- About 15 of 49 pages are mostly empty or dividers, so it feels padded.
- The "dashboard" mention feels like a teaser for something I didn't get.

What would make it feel worth *more*:
- A 5-minute screen recording: free ChatGPT on an iPhone, from paste to report.
- Bank-by-bank "where are my statements" cards for the top 10 US banks and cards.
- Screenshots using the fictional family's real numbers, so I can see what good output looks like.
- An editable worksheet (Google Sheets / Numbers / Notes template).
- A "your first 3 actions after the report" page, e.g. "cancel X, call about fee Y, set autopay".
- If the dashboard exists, include it. If not, cut the reference.

## 6. My top 10 changes (most important first)

1. **p10: Remove the placeholder and add the real screenshots.** Replace "[Screenshots coming: finding statements in a banking app, and downloading a CSV on the bank's website]" with actual iPhone screenshots of finding and saving a statement PDF, and say plainly whether CSV needs a computer. Right now it's the most important step and the only unfinished one.

2. **p13-15 / p20: Show the free plan on an iPhone, since that's what most buyers use.** "The screenshots in Part 2 come from a real run on ChatGPT's paid plan" tells free users their experience isn't shown. Add at least: the free-plan "Next: Visa and Amex. Reply go" message, the upload-limit message and what to do, and the chat-limit message. Then run it end to end on a real free account and update p35's table ("Without code (free-plan style)") to include it.

3. **p8 / p13: Say exactly which files the customer has and where.** "The PDF and text file that came with this guide" and "attach the prompt PDF" should become: "Your download has 2 files: Money_XRay_Guide.pdf (this) and Money_XRay_Prompt.txt. On iPhone, open the .txt in Files, tap Select All, Copy."

4. **p11: Give exact privacy steps for ChatGPT on iPhone.** Replace "Look in the app's settings under privacy or data controls ... check them yourself rather than relying on a screenshot" with the actual taps, then "Names change; if yours looks different, look for 'Data Controls'." Also add "Your privacy" to the "fastest path" on p4: "read chapter 4, then 5, then 6".

5. **p16, p18, p21, p27: Use the fictional family's numbers in the screenshots, not blacked-out ones, and make them match the text.** I can't learn what a $0.00 Check looks like from grey boxes. Fix the mismatches ("2. Transaction reconciliation" vs "How much to trust this"; "10. Information requiring clarification" vs "never more than 8"; the p21 save block that doesn't match the p49 format; 26/27 vs 23/23 months).

6. **Move the payoff to the front.** Put a one-page excerpt of the sample report (p36-37), "Reading 'left over'" (p17), and the net worth line "Worth over $160,000 on paper, with almost nothing free once the cards are paid" (p29) right after p4. That's the "this is me" moment, and it's on page 36.

7. **p44: Move "Your checklist" to page 5.** It's the whole process on one page, and it's what I'd screenshot and follow.

8. **p47: Remove or explain "Notion export ... for the Money X-Ray dashboard".** There is no dashboard in what I bought. Either include it or cut menu option 4 from the prompt.

9. **p8 / p20: Make the free-plan time estimate honest.** "About 20 minutes" / "Longer on a free plan" should become something like "Free plan with 5 accounts: plan on two sittings. Tonight, upload your main checking and main card; tomorrow, the rest." Also explain "Paid plans ... can run the math in code" in one plain sentence so free users don't think they're getting worse math.

10. **Tighten the layout: p5/12/22/32 dividers, and p7, 9, 19, 21, 31, 34, 38, 40, 42 near-empty pages; fix the p16 clipped captions and the p48 overflowing line.** Drop or shrink the dividers, stop tables splitting for one row, and give the worksheets (p39-42) an editable Sheets/Numbers/Notes version. As it stands, about a third of the pages feel like padding, and that's what makes $39 feel steep.
