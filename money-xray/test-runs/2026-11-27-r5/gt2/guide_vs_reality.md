## Mismatches and gaps

1. **p33 to p35 (ch. 19) and p29: the sample report is this same test household, and its category table breaks the prompt's own cash rule.** p34 shows Groceries $572.44, Shopping $475.87 and Dining out $312.48, with the ATM cash left in "Everything else". After the customer explains the ATM cash, the prompt's rule 3 for cash answers moves it into categories. That gives Groceries $585.78, Shopping $495.87, Dining Out $325.82 and Childcare $190.00. The p29 budget example then calls Dining $280 "about 10% below average". Against the correct $325.82 average, $280 is 14% below. A customer comparing their report to the sample sees different numbers and can't tell why. It is also a test-design problem: the guide prints the expected answers for the exact dataset used to test it (p18 says the prompt "was improved against" this household).
2. **p8 to p9 (ch. 4), Android: almost nothing.** All five screenshots are iPhone ("Save to Files", "iCloud Drive"). Android gets one sentence on p9: "use Download or Share, then save to your files." The guide never says where Android downloads land (the Downloads folder or Files by Google), how to rename files to the suggested "Chase checking Jan" style, or that many bank apps open PDFs in a viewer that only offers Share. The p13 file-picker screenshots are iOS too. On Android, selecting several files at once in the AI app's picker usually means going through "Browse" or Files, and the guide doesn't mention it.
3. **p9 and p10 (ch. 4 and 5): card balances push Android users toward PDFs they can't easily redact.** p9 says card CSVs lack balances, so "upload the PDF statements". p10 says to cover the name and account number on PDFs "with your phone's markup tool". Most Android phones have no built-in PDF markup or redaction (Google Photos Markup works on images, not PDFs). In this run the balances came from a separate `statement_summaries.csv`. The guide never describes such a file or how to type in balances instead (for example, "tell it the opening and closing balance", which the prompt allows). Card CSVs also lack the APR and minimum, and the guide doesn't tell people to have them ready, so the AI estimated the Visa APR at 23.7% when it was really 22.99%.
4. **Kit p2: "most phones' Files or Books apps work too" is iPhone-only.** Books is an Apple app, and Files by Google can't fill PDF forms. An Android path (for example the Drive PDF viewer or Adobe Acrobat Reader) isn't named. On a paid plan this matters when the person wants to type into the leftover boxes (actual spending, cancelled-on dates, ticks). Kit p2 step 3 says "keep it on your phone" without saying where the filled PDF lands on Android. I couldn't check whether Android viewers show the filled values. The PDF relies on NeedAppearances; it rendered correctly in MuPDF, but some mobile viewers show blank fields.
5. **p21 (ch. 11): cancelling on Android isn't explained.** "Cancel in your phone's subscription settings" doesn't give the Android path (Play Store, profile, Payments & subscriptions). For this customer it's wrong anyway: the cut items bill as APPLE.COM/BILL and HULU on cards, so they must be cancelled with Apple (apple.com or the Apple Music app account settings) and on hulu.com. Play Store subscriptions won't list them, and the guide doesn't say how to tell which is which (look at the merchant name on the statement).
6. **p17 and Kit p2: where "fill my kit" fits in the flow is unclear.** The p17 menu lists 7 next to 1 to 5 and says to "include what each pick needs in the same reply". The Action Kit paragraph says "Upload it in the same chat and type fill my kit". It doesn't say whether to attach the kit to the 1 to 5 reply or send it afterwards. The prompt wants it done after the other picks. The customer here sent it as a separate message, which worked, but the guide should say so.
7. **Kit p16 to p17 versus the kit's own layout.** The 25-character limit squeezes wide boxes: "Accounts it couldn't see" (p3) had to read "Discover card; savings" in a box built for a sentence, and the "Top 3 things to fix" lines were cut down the same way. The emergency fund block (Kit p12) stays empty in every standard run, because the field list says "empty if they gave no goal" and neither the guide nor the menu ever asks for one. The 12-week challenge (Kit p13) jumps to $300 although the cuts freed only $29.98 a month ($89.94 for 3 months), because of the "at least $300" rule. The guide doesn't explain that.
8. **p7 and p12: "the text file copies most cleanly on a phone."** Many Android phones have no default .txt viewer that allows select-all and copy of 25 KB. Attaching the .txt (the other option on p12) works. The guide should recommend attaching on Android.
9. **p17 Spreadsheet export.** With code, three files came back (transactions, debts, net worth). The guide only mentions "one CSV file with every account". It doesn't say how to get the files from the chat into Notion, Google Sheets or Excel on Android: download, then import from Drive or Files. The customer's earlier answer "I want to use the Notion dashboard" gets no Notion-specific help (Notion's Android app can't import a CSV as a database; that takes the web or desktop app).
10. **p4: "about 20 to 30 minutes on a paid plan" and p19 "copy it into your notes app".** I couldn't time it here. The full save block is about 3,500 characters. That's fine for Google Keep, but on a phone it's a long-press-and-drag selection unless the app has a Copy button on code blocks. The guide doesn't mention that button.

## Smaller notes
- p12 and p16 screenshots come from an older prompt; the guide says so. The p16 caption is accurate.
- p25: the worked example's "family loan" is $450. CUSTOMER.md also says "$600 I borrowed". Not a guide error, but a real user could give both numbers. The prompt has no rule for conflicting balances.
- Option 6 (PDF report) and option 8 (monthly checkup) weren't exercised in this run.
