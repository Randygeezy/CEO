Page numbers are the printed page numbers in Money_XRay_Complete_Guide.pdf. The prompt was followed as written; "reality" below is what the prompt made the assistant do.

## Things that confused the customer

- **Step 1 wording vs. guide**: guide p.13 says paste the prompt and send "Start". That's fine, but p.14 says type "done" after uploading. The customer uploaded everything at once and typed "done" anyway; the prompt only requires "done" for batches. No harm, just an unclear rule.
- **The summary file**: the 6th upload (statement_summaries.csv) isn't an account. The guide (p.10, "Include the balance") says CSVs often have a balance column and the PDF helps otherwise, but never mentions a separate summary file. The assistant handled it fine.
- **Mom's loan amount**: in the cash answer the customer said "$600 I borrowed"; later (net worth) "I owe my mom $450". The assistant had to show "Borrowed $600; current balance?" until the second answer. The guide's cash example (p.19) doesn't cover loans repaid in cash.
- **The customer script referred to an interview that never happened**: the net-worth answer in the script says "(loan $14,600 as I said)", but under this prompt the customer never said it (there's no interview). I changed it to "(loan $14,600 at 6.9%)". Similarly, the customer's real Visa APR (22.99%) and minimum ($195) and the goal of "$300 a month to free up" were never asked for, so they never entered the run.
- **"Paid in full" cards**: the report says the Mastercard and Amex "charged no interest, so they aren't debt", yet they carry ~$1,113 and ~$646 and get partial-looking payments ($450 / $875 / $450; $250 / $825 / $400). A customer may read that as contradictory. Net worth correctly counts them as owed.
- **Guide p.27 and p.29 show this household's exact results** (lump $2,587.08, net worth $162,554, liquid $154) as a "fictional family". This run independently matched p.29 ($162,554.08 / $154.08) and p.25's fee total ($522.59), which is reassuring. But a customer comparing would wonder why the free plan gives no "23 months / $2,007".
- **Visa plan warning**: the plan's Visa payment ($553.84) is lower than what they pay the Visa today ($750 to $875) because today's payments also cover about $794 a month of new purchases. The guide (p.28) warns to stop new charges but doesn't explain that the planned payment can be lower than the current one; a customer could read it as "pay less".
- **Several save blocks**: the customer had six "copy this into a note" instructions and no guidance (p.20-21) on replacing the earlier ones.
- **Privacy detail**: the assistant removed a Delta ticket number from the exported description. The guide's privacy chapter (p.11) only mentions account numbers, names and addresses, so the edited "raw" description may surprise the customer.
