# The Fallowford Forge: a six-book plan for a cozy midlife fantasy

This plan turns the best bet in [Underserved novel niches 2026](https://github.com/Randygeezy/CEO/blob/claude/bold-volta-fd8f7v/reports/Underserved%20novel%20niches%202026.md) into a series you can start writing. It combines the report's rank 1 gap, a midlife woman lead, with its rank 3 category, cozy crafting fantasy. That category has the strongest measured economics in the report and the least AI dilution. **The series follows Marta Kell, 52, a lapsed journeyman smith who goes home to relight her mother's forge and keep a dying crossroads town on the map, one commission at a time.** Season One has six books of 65,000 to 70,000 words, released between April and December 2027. After the first two, a new book comes out every eight weeks. Romance is light, slow and closed-door. There is no spice and no game system. Progression shows in guild ranks, tools, forge upgrades and a town ledger at the end of each book.

One thing has changed since the report: **the midlife lane is not empty.** Paranormal women's fiction (PWF) already serves midlife heroines with long, humorous series such as *Magical Midlife Madness* and *Magic After Midlife* ([Rising Shadow](https://www.risingshadow.net/book/65155-magical-midlife-madness); [BingeBooks](https://bingebooks.com/book/magic-after-midlife)). At least two cozy fantasy series with older leads have been publishing through 2025 and 2026: Kirsten Marion's Rowanswood Crones, about a 60-year-old widow who discovers she is the Fire Crone ([Kirkus](https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/kirsten-marion/the-chic-crone-a-paranormal-womens-fantasy-the-row/); [NetGalley](https://www.netgalley.com/catalog/book/717771)), and Julia Huni's Dragons of Safe Haven, which opens with *A Dragon Rider's Guide to Retirement* ([Rising Shadow](https://risingshadow.net/book/83274-a-dragon-riders-guide-to-retirement)). That shows readers are there. It also means this series has to stand apart clearly, so the one-afternoon validation check below comes before any drafting.

## The series engine

Each book is one season in Fallowford. Marta learns one new tier of the craft and the town earns one Waystation Article. Each book also follows one life-stage thread, builds to a set piece where the whole town works together, and closes with the Fallowford Ledger.

**The Waystation Articles.** The peace treaty reopens the Salt Road, and the Salt Road Company will charter only towns that meet its six Articles. Fallowford starts with none. Each book earns one: a licensed smith (Book 1), stabling and farriery (2), a signal bell (3), secure winter stores (4), a flood-proof crossing (5), and a master smith in residence (6). The list is the season's spine, and readers can track it.

**Setting, the series' magic.** A master who knows her craft completely can sometimes *set* one intention into a piece at the moment it is finished. Set work does its job a little better than it should: a tire holds true on any road, and a bell carries to anyone who left. Setting can't be forced or faked, most masters never manage it, and the Guild records every set piece. Marta sets her first piece by accident in Book 1. Learning to do it on purpose is the season's magic progression.

**Clinker.** A forge salamander who has been asleep in the ash since the forge went cold six years ago. He doesn't talk. His flame shows his mood: blue when content, orange when annoyed, white when proud. He grows as the forge and the town recover, so he mirrors the progression.

**Agathe's Rules.** Each chapter opens with a line from her mother's forge notebook. The lines carry Agathe's voice even after she is gone, and they make good quote cards for marketing.

1. Iron first. Wishes after.
2. Let the hammer fall. You're not pushing a cart.
3. Never quench in anger.
4. Charge for the work. Give the advice away; nobody takes it anyway.
5. If you can't hear the iron, you're talking too much.
6. Old hands are slow. Slow hands are true.
7. Everything rusts that isn't loved.
8. A smith who hurries makes nails. A smith who waits makes bells.

**The Fallowford Ledger.** Marta kept her husband's business books for 25 years, and now she keeps the town's. The final page of each book is her ledger. It gives crafting readers their numbers without a game screen. Book 1 ends like this:

```
THE FALLOWFORD LEDGER, Midsummer, first year of the reopened road
Kept by M. Kell, journeyman (reinstated)

Souls in town ........ 47 (+6)
Forge ................ relit. Hearth relined. Bellows: new leather.
Salamander ........... awake (blue, mostly)
Tools made ........... 1 hammer, 2 lb 6 oz, to my hand · 2 pairs of tongs
Work done ............ 1,140 nails · 38 hinges · 11 plowshares · 1 axe (T. Varga, steel bit, sound) · 1 wagon tire (set)
Waystation Articles .. Smith ✓ · Stabling ✗ · Bell ✗ · Stores ✗ · Crossing ✗ · Master ✗
Owed to us ........... 14 silver (Sallowmere coal factor, for once)
Owed by us ........... everything else
```

## Content promise

- **Closed door.** No sex on the page, no spice and no love triangle.
- **No gore.** No battles and no animal deaths. The dangers are weather, deadlines, money and pride.
- **The aging body is on the page.** Hot flashes, waking at 3 a.m., bad knees and reading glasses are written frankly and with humor. Marta can joke about them; the narrative never treats her as the joke.
- **Antagonists have reasons.** Most of them come around.
- **Grief is gentle.** Agathe dies in Book 6 at a great age, in her own chair, after seeing her daughter made a master. Give that book a short content note up front.

## World and cast

**Fallowford** is a hill town where the old Salt Road crosses the River Fallow at a ford. It was a busy caravan stop with three smithies until a border war closed the pass for nine years. Then the population fell from about 600 to about 40. Down the valley, the canal town of **Sallowmere** prospered and wants the reopened road's traffic. Marta spent 30 years in the capital, **Varrow**. Under the reed beds lies the town's forgotten wealth, Fallow-marsh bog iron, which is revealed in Book 6.

| Character | Age | Role | Season arc |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marta Kell | 52 | Lapsed journeyman smith; did a cloth merchant's books for 25 years | Stops being useful to everyone else and makes something of her own. Goes from journeyman to master. |
| Agathe Kell | 79 | Her mother, Fallowford's smith for 55 years. Her eyes are clouded but her mind is sharp | Teaches against the clock. Reconciles with Marta. Dies at peace in Book 6. |

## Book 1: *Embers & Empty Nests*

**Logline:** A 52-year-old smith goes home to relight her mother's cold forge. She has until Midsummer to win back her license, or the reopened road and the town's future go to Sallowmere.

**Shape:** 70,000 words in 24 chapters of about 2,900 words each. The story runs from early spring to Midsummer. Two deadlines meet at the end: the Guild assessment and the Company's first caravan.

### Act One: the cold forge (chapters 1 to 6)

1. **The Letter.** It's 3 a.m. in Varrow. Marta is awake with a hot flash in the rented rooms she moved into when her marriage ended. Teo left last month to tour with a band, and she's still doing Corwin's books out of habit. Her mother's letter is four lines: *The forge is cold. The road opens at Midsummer. The Guild wants a smith or they'll give the crossroads to Sallowmere. Come home if you're coming.* By breakfast she has packed.
2. **The Salt Road.** Two days on a carter's wagon bring her to a half-empty town. The inn is shuttered, the ford is quiet, and most chimneys are cold. Agathe sits by the cold forge. "You took your time." "Thirty years." "I meant this week."
3. **Cold Iron.** In daylight the forge is a ruin: the hearth is cracked, the bellows are rotten and the anvil is rusted. Clinker's ember lies dark in the ash. Marta's first heat in thirty years gives her a crooked nail, a blister and a hot flash she can blame on the fire for once.
4. **The Reeve's Ledger.** At a town meeting in the Crossed Keys, Reeve Holloway lays out the stakes. To become a waystation, Fallowford needs a Guild-licensed smith by Midsummer, and Sallowmere has offered to "cover" the town. Nobody trusts "Agathe's girl, the one who left" until Marta reads the reeve's ledger and finds 14 silver the Sallowmere coal factor has owed the town since before the war. Her bookkeeping years turn out to count.
5. **Charcoal.** The coal factor still won't pay the 14 silver, and he wants cash up front from a lapsed journeyman. Tilly Varga trades a winter's charcoal for a new felling axe, due at Midsummer. Marta can't make that axe yet.
6. **The Guild's Letter.** To be reinstated, Marta must pass a journeyman's assessment at Midsummer before Assessor Edric Pell. Sallowmere's Garrick Hale is competing for the same license. She puts on her mother's old apron. It fits differently now.

### Act Two, first half: relearning (chapters 7 to 12)

7. **Bellows.** Marta and Hob reline the hearth and replaces the bellows leather. The first paid work comes in: hinges for the inn's shutters and nails for three roofs. Each repair opens another window in town.
8. **Striker.** Teo arrives broke after the band splits. Marta had only just got free of looking after people, but she hands him the sledge anyway. He's hopeless at first, then finds the rhythm. Hammer and sledge turn out to work like music.
9. **Three in the Morning.** Mother and daughter, both awake at 3 a.m., talk honestly for the first time in thirty years. They talk about why Marta left, why Agathe never asked her back, and Agathe's eleven years of hot flashes ("I used to stand in the snow and call it fresh air").
10. **The Assessor.** Pell makes a surprise inspection and logs thirty-one regulation breaches. He also writes one line in his book: "nails: true." Garrick, charming and cocky, offers to buy Agathe's tools.
11. **Plowshares.** Spring planting can't wait for a license. Marta reforges eleven plowshares in nine days and the fields get planted. Her shoulder tears on the last one.
12. **Let the Hammer Fall.** *(Midpoint.)* With one good arm, Marta can't work the way she did at 22. Agathe can no longer see fine detail, but she still knows technique, and she teaches the old way: leverage, timing, and a lighter hammer made for Marta's own hand. Marta forges that hammer. It's the first tool she has made for herself, and in the ash Clinker sparks once.

### Act Two, second half: pressure (chapters 13 to 18)

13. **Lise.** Her daughter arrives from Varrow. She has lined up a respectable bookkeeping post for her mother and plans to take her back to the city. She finds a woman with burned forearms who looks happy, and doesn't know what to do with that.
14. **Sallowmere.** At Garrick's guild smithy, with its rolling mill and six journeymen, he makes Marta an offer: sell him Fallowford's license claim and keep his books. She buys steel for Tilly's axe and leaves angrier and clearer.
15. **Tilly's Axe.** Forge-welding a steel bit into an iron head fails twice. The third time, Teo strikes and Agathe calls the heats by sound, and the weld takes. Tilly takes Marta to a night charcoal burn, and they become friends.
16. **High Water.** A spring storm floods the lower town and soaks the charcoal. Agathe falls, and Marta stays at her bedside instead of the forge. Two weeks before Midsummer, she hasn't started the test piece, and Lise's offer is still open.
17. **Thirty Tags.** Clearing the back room, Marta finds her own journeyman's tools, oiled and wrapped. Each carries a cloth tag in Agathe's hand, one for every winter she was gone. Neither of them mentions it. Agathe only says: "Everything rusts that isn't loved."
18. **The Town Turns Out.** Tilly brings dry charcoal from her own stock, the Ferrises feed the house and neighbors bring scrap iron. Lise takes over the reeve's letters to the Company. Marta tells her daughter she's staying.

### Act Three: Midsummer (chapters 19 to 24)

19. **The Test Piece.** Strap hinges and a thumb latch for the inn's great door. It's plain work, so it has to be perfect. Teo strikes, Agathe listens from her chair, and Marta finishes a day early.
20. **Midsummer Eve.** Pell and Garrick arrive, and Fallowford holds its first fair in nine years. The Company's first caravan is due in the morning. Its report will decide whether Fallowford becomes a waystation.
21. **The Broken Wheel.** The caravan comes in early through a storm, and a potter family's wagon splits its tire on the ford stones. Without a new iron tire by dawn, the family is stranded and the caravan writes Fallowford off. Garrick says it can't be done without a proper tire-setting platform. Marta's test piece is finished, so she picks up the traveler wheel, the tool for measuring a tire.
22. **Tyring.** At dawn the whole town stands around a ring fire holding buckets. Teo and, to everyone's surprise, Garrick lift the glowing tire onto the wheel. As it's quenched, Marta feels something go into the iron, *hold true*. In the forge, Clinker wakes in a burst of blue flame. Ansel Dray watches from the wagon bench. (The iron came from the town's old scrap, which foreshadows Book 6.)
23. **Assessment.** Pell passes her hinges and reinstates her as a journeyman. Then he reports what he saw at dawn: the tire is *set*, and only masters set. He can't make her a master, but he writes a recommendation for master's candidacy within the year. The Company names Fallowford a provisional waystation, pending the five Articles still missing.
24. **The Ledger.** The inn reopens on Marta's hinges. The caravan leaves with a promise to return. Lise goes home, promising to come back for the harvest, and Teo stays. At 3 a.m., mother and daughter sit by a warm forge with Clinker asleep in the coals. Agathe says: "Iron first, wishes after. You did both. Don't make a habit of it." The book closes with the Fallowford Ledger.

| 3 | *Bells & Borrowed Time* | Autumn | Bronze founding | Signal bell | An aging parent; the sandwich years |
| 4 | *Locks & Long Nights* | Midwinter (the holiday book) | Locks and fine mechanisms | Secure winter stores | A holiday with the grown kids and the ex |
| 5 | *Rivets & Rising Water* | Spring flood | Structural ironwork: chain, rivets, a bridge | Flood-proof crossing | Becoming a grandmother; a second, chosen empty nest |
| 6 | *Masterworks & Mothers* | Early summer to Midsummer | Smelting: bog ore to bloom to steel | Master smith in residence | Losing a mother; claiming her own name |

**Book 2, *Horseshoes & Hot Flashes*.** Caravans now stop every week. Marta was never a farrier, but she has to shoe forty teams, and Teo turns out to have his grandmother's way with horses. A heat wave hits during the worst of her perimenopause, when she's sleepless, foggy and furious, and her first deliberate set is for herself: hairpins that hold the river's cold. In a small cozy mystery, someone is sabotaging wagons on the pass road to push the Company toward Sallowmere. The culprit is a desperate teamster, not Garrick. Ansel's caravan lays over for two weeks, and he and Marta spend evenings on the inn porch. *Hook:* autumn fog nearly loses a caravan at the ford, and the Company wants a signal bell by first snow.

**Book 3, *Bells & Borrowed Time*.** Fallowford can't afford bronze, so every household gives something to the melt. One gives a candlestick, one a soldier's medal, and Ansel quietly gives his late wife's ring. Agathe cast the town's old bell before the war melted it down. Now she is failing, and she races to teach Marta founding. The first casting cracks. The second rings true, set with *come home*, and it carries to people who left Fallowford, Agathe's estranged sister among them. *Hook:* the uplands' harvest fails, a hungry winter is coming, and the Company wants secure stores.

**Book 4, *Locks & Long Nights*.** Snow traps the caravan goods in the granary, and the town has a thief. The thief is a hungry upland family hiding in the old tannery. Their eldest, Wren, picked Marta's first lock. Marta's final lock carries a set the Guild would call foolish: *open for the hungry*. Lise, her husband and Corwin are snowed in for Longest Night. Corwin gets on far too well with Ansel, and Lise announces she's pregnant. Marta and Ansel stop pretending. They kiss, and the door closes. *Hook:* the thaw means the ford will flood, and the Company wants a crossing that holds.

**Book 5, *Rivets & Rising Water*.** Marta races the snowmelt to build an iron-strapped bridge. Garrick works beside her as a colleague, with Pell signing off. Teo leaves with Ansel's caravan as its farrier, so the nest empties again, this time by choice. Lise goes into labor and arrives on the night the flood peaks. The bridge, set with *carry them over*, holds. Marta becomes a grandmother that night, with four generations of women under one roof. *Hook:* the Guild summons Marta to the Midsummer Fair for master's candidacy, and a master's mark must be struck from steel the candidate made from ore.

**Book 6, *Masterworks & Mothers*.** Agathe's last lesson is the old bloomery her grandmother used. The town pumps bellows through the night to smelt Fallow-marsh bog iron. The steel takes a set like nothing in the Guild halls, which explains the Book 1 tire and shows that the town's lost wealth was under its feet all along. In Varrow, among her old life and her ex's new one, Marta presents a masterpiece the judges laugh at: a bread knife. It cuts the fair's bread, and the bread feeds everyone. She earns her touchmark, her official maker's mark, with Agathe in the hall. Agathe dies at home days later in her chair by the forge, with Clinker in her lap. Marta strikes her first touchmark on her mother's marker and signs Wren's apprenticeship. Ansel takes the Company's post at Fallowford. *Season Two hook:* everyone now wants fallow steel.

## Titles, blurb and cover

**Series:** The Fallowford Forge. **Subtitle for every book:** *A Cozy Midlife Fantasy*.

Every title follows the "Craft & Life" pattern. The ampersand pairing signals cozy fantasy at a glance. The first word names the craft tier and the second names the midlife truth, so the underserved protagonist appears in the title itself, as the report recommends. *Horseshoes & Hot Flashes* is meant to be blunt: menopause is the sharpest gap in the evidence. A web search on November 17, 2026 found no book with either of the first two titles and no fantasy called Fallowford.

KDP's rules are short. Title plus subtitle must be 200 characters or fewer. The subtitle and series name follow the same rules as the title, which forbid unauthorized references to other titles or authors ([KDP metadata guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201097560)). Keep comp authors out of every metadata field, and print the subtitle on the cover so it is part of the real title.

**Blurb (Book 1):**

> **She gave up the forge to raise a family. At fifty-two, she's taking it back.**
>
> Marta Kell's children are grown, her marriage ended kindly, and for the first time in thirty years nobody needs her anywhere. Then a four-line letter arrives from her mother: *The forge is cold. The road opens at Midsummer. Come home if you're coming.*
>
> Fallowford was a busy crossroads once. Now the war-closed Salt Road is reopening, and the Smiths' Guild has made the dwindling town an offer: put a licensed smith at the crossroads by Midsummer, or the caravans, and the town's future, go to Sallowmere down the valley.
>
> Marta's journeyman papers lapsed before her daughter could walk. Her shoulders remember the hammer. Her knees have opinions. The hearth is cracked, the coal man wants cash, the Guild's assessor thinks she's a joke, and the little fire salamander who sleeps in the ashes hasn't woken in six years.
>
> But Marta has run a household, a merchant's books and three decades of other people's emergencies. A cold forge doesn't scare her. Her mother might.
>
> *Embers & Empty Nests* is a warm, wry cozy fantasy about starting over in the middle, for readers who loved *Legends & Lattes* and wished the heroine had a few more years on her. No spice, no dark lords, real smithing, and one very opinionated salamander.

**Ad hooks to test:**

1. "Her kids left. Her marriage ended kindly. Her mother's forge went cold. At fifty-two, Marta Kell is lighting it again."
2. "A cozy fantasy heroine with reading glasses, hot flashes and a hammer made to fit her hand."
3. "She's not the chosen one. She's the only smith for forty miles."

**Cover brief:**

- Use an illustrated, warm, painterly style. Marta stands at the center and is clearly in her fifties: silver-streaked hair in a working knot, reading glasses pushed up on her head, laugh lines, strong forearms and a leather apron.
- She holds tongs with a glowing hinge or horseshoe. Clinker glows blue on the anvil. Behind her are the ford, the crossroads sign and a few lit windows.
- The title is hand-lettered, with the subtitle on the cover.
- Every book uses the same layout, with one craft object and one seasonal palette each: spring green and ember (1), harvest gold (2), russet and bronze (3), snow blue and brass (4), rain teal and iron (5), and midsummer rose and bright steel (6).
- The non-negotiable: **she must look her age.** The age is the hook, so if she looks 30, the cover has failed. Make sure the cover doesn't look like a crafting LitRPG (weapon, stat panel, young man) or like the PWF covers you will survey during validation.

**Metadata starting points.** Pick three categories in KDP, with Cozy Fantasy first. For the other two, copy the strongest categories shown on comps' product pages, such as *The Chic Crone* and *A Dragon Rider's Guide to Retirement*. A women's-fiction family or friendship category is a natural third. Draft keywords: "older heroine fantasy," "midlife woman fantasy," "female blacksmith fantasy," "progression fantasy no system," "small town found family fantasy," "clean cozy fantasy no spice" and "menopause fiction." Check each against Amazon's search autocomplete before you use it.

## Release calendar

This calendar assumes one writer drafting 2,000 new words a day, five days a week. At that pace a 65,000-word book takes about 6.5 weeks to draft plus 1.5 weeks to revise, or 8 weeks per book. An editor works on each finished draft for about three weeks while the next book is drafted. At half that pace, bank Books 1 to 3 before launching and accept about four months between later books, or bring in a co-writer.

| Nov 30, 2026 to Feb 7, 2027 | Book 1, 70,000 words (10 weeks including the holidays) | Set up the newsletter and reader magnet |
| Feb 8 to Apr 4 | Book 2 | Book 1 to the editor, then proofreading; ARCs out Mar 15 |

Book 2 is fully drafted and with the editor when Book 1 launches, so readers who finish Book 1 can go straight to a pre-order that carries no deadline risk. Book 4 is the winter book, so run a Book 1 price promotion every December and point it at Book 4.

**Launch setup**

- **Kindle+ for the ebooks** (KDP Select). In the report, 78 of the top 100 fantasy titles were in the subscription.
- **Price:** Book 1 at $2.99, the bottom of KDP's 70% royalty band, and Books 2 to 6 at $4.99.
- **Print:** a paperback for every book, plus a large-print paperback of Book 1 at least, since older readers are the core large-print market. Large print is a separate edition with its own ISBN. Guides describe a Large Print checkbox in KDP's paperback setup, so confirm it in your dashboard ([ALLi](https://selfpublishingadvice.org/?p=184269); [Kindlepreneur](https://kindlepreneur.com/large-print/)).
- **Reader magnet:** *The Smith's Daughter*, a 10,000-word prequel from Agathe's point of view on the night 22-year-old Marta leaves. Give it away only to newsletter subscribers and keep it out of KDP Select, which requires digital exclusivity for enrolled books.
- **Back matter:** a link to the next book, a newsletter sign-up and Agathe's Rules.
- **ARCs:** 20 to 40 readers who match the target reader, sent four weeks before launch.
- **Ads:** start once Book 2 is out, because read-through is what makes ads pay. Use Amazon product targeting on the comps and Meta interest targeting for PWF and cozy fantasy readers. Test the three hooks above against each other.
- **Audio:** Kindle+ now pays eligible audiobooks from the same fund as page reads. Decide on audio after Book 3, when post-October payouts have settled.
- **AI disclosure:** KDP has asked publishers to disclose AI-generated text, images and translations since 2023, so check the current wording. Part of this niche's case is that human-written books hold up where AI dilution is low. Real craft detail and lived midlife experience are the moat.

**Gate 1: beta read of Book 1 (February 2027).** Use six to eight readers who match the target: women of about 45 to 70 who read cozy fantasy or PWF. Pass if most finish and ask what happens next. Fix anything two or more of them flag.

**Gate 2: read-through, 60 days after Book 2 launches (mid-July 2027).** Estimate full reads for each book by dividing its Kindle+ pages read by its page count (KENPC), then add sales. Divide Book 2's total by Book 1's ([method](https://kindlepreneur.com/calculate-series-read-through/)). Practitioner blogs, which don't share their methods, call 40 to 65% healthy for fantasy and treat anything under 30% as a problem with Book 1. If you're under 30%, fix Book 1's ending hook and pacing, and hold ad spend until it improves.

**Gate 3: Season Two (after Book 5 launches, November 2027).** If read-through holds from Book 2 onward and Book 1 keeps selling or being borrowed without heavy ads, outline Season Two. If not, finish Book 6 and stop there. The season is designed to end cleanly.

## Risks

- **The lane fills while you write.** Midlife cozy fantasy is attracting authors now. The defenses are the validation gate, sharp differentiation and an April launch with Book 2 already done.
- **The cadence breaks the writer or the books.** Protect the first two releases. Let later gaps stretch to ten weeks before you cut the editing.
- **Kindle+ income is unsettled.** Authors reported KENP estimates falling 40 to 70% after the October 22, 2026 change (anecdotal). Model income on sales plus conservative page reads, and keep the print editions.
- **The cover reads as PWF or LitRPG.** Show it to ARC readers or test it in ads before locking the series design.
- **Menopause humor punches down.** Beta readers from the target demographic catch this. Marta jokes about it; the book never does.
- **Smithing errors.** Crafting readers notice. See the research list below.

## Getting the craft right

- Alex W. Bealer, *The Art of Blacksmithing*: forge welding, heats and the traditional tools.
- Alexander G. Weygers, *The Complete Modern Blacksmith*: making your own tools, which covers Marta's Book 1 hammer.
- George Sturt, *The Wheelwright's Shop* (1923): how iron tires were set on wooden wheels, for chapter 22.
- A one-day introductory blacksmithing class, for the heat, the noise and how a shoulder feels after an hour.
- Before outlining Books 3 and 6, watch a bell-casting demonstration and an experimental-archaeology bloomery smelt. Both are well documented.
