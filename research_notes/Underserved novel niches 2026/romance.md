# Underserved Romance Niches (Reader Demand vs. Supply), as of Nov 2026

> **Method and access caveat (read first).** In this session, reddit.com was blocked for both WebSearch and direct fetch. Substack, swoonscript.com and theeverygirl.com could not be fetched either (DNS blocked). So **no Reddit threads, upvote counts or reply counts could be collected directly.** Everything below comes from search-engine summaries of pages. Short quoted phrases are reproduced as they appeared in those summaries. They are **not** verified against the full page and may be lightly paraphrased. Many sources are author blogs, newsletters or promotional "demand data" vendors (PlotProse/TropeSmith, Fractal Enigma, Monster Romance Reviews), so treat them as directional. Before acting on any niche, someone with Reddit access should check it directly: search r/RomanceBooks, r/MonsterRomance, r/Romantasy and r/DarkRomance for "recs" and "wish there were more" posts from the past year, sorted by top.

## 1. Which subgenres and trope combinations are readers repeatedly requesting in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
The best-sourced "readers ask, authors don't write" signals are: (a) **monster romance outside orcs**, especially Greek-myth creatures (satyr/fawn, "cozy" satyr, minotaur in a dairy/milking setup), sea creatures and demons; (b) **older heroines, 40+**, in both contemporary romance and romantasy; (c) **dark romance outside the mafia setting**; (d) **historical romance outside Regency** (Victorian, Gilded Age to 1950s); (e) **M/M sports romance outside hockey** and sports romance with a female athlete who actually plays. Most of this evidence is commentary or aggregated data, not primary reader threads.

### Cited Findings
**Monster romance (strongest niche-level evidence)**
- A Substack gap analysis, "The Greek Monster Gap: What Readers Want and Authors Aren't Writing", combines a Kickstarter case study with Reddit sentiment analysis. It flags the **dairy/milking theme** as open territory and says readers want **"cozy" satyr stories and fawn romance**. It also notes that readers keep referring back to the original myths. The post is about 355 days old (late 2025) — [Monster Romance Reviews](https://monsterromancereviews.substack.com/p/the-greek-monster-gap-what-readers)
- The same post calls **minotaur** "the most established" Greek-monster niche. *Morning Glory Milking Farm* appears "across multiple threads as the minotaur romance reference point". The post also says orc romance "looks mainstream" by comparison — [Monster Romance Reviews](https://monsterromancereviews.substack.com/p/the-greek-monster-gap-what-readers)
- **M/M orc romance gap:** an October 2026 market report says five of the top dozen orc romance results are Finley Fenn's Orc Sworn books. As of Oct 20, 2026, "none of the M/M search's top 25 results was M/M orc romance". (The newsletter sells paid guides.) — [Monster Romance Reviews, "Horde Mentality"](https://monsterromancereviews.substack.com/p/horde-mentality-whos-filling-the)
- **Sea-creature romance (2026):** many blurbs leave out trope keywords readers search for. For example, "enemies to lovers" appears in 67 Reddit mentions but only 5 blurbs — [Monster Romance Reviews](https://monsterromancereviews.substack.com/p/plenty-of-data-in-the-sea)
- **Demon romance:** the report finds a "discovery gap": only 4 of 67 bestselling Amazon demon-romance authors appear in the social discussions it examined. The most reliable tropes are slow burn, forced proximity and fated mates — [Monster Romance Reviews](https://monsterromancereviews.substack.com/p/the-devil-is-in-the-data)
- A survey of 43 monster romance authors found that most started as readers who couldn't find the story they wanted — [Monster Romance Reviews](https://monsterromancereviews.substack.com/p/43-authors-one-year-zero-normal-answers)

**Older heroines (contemporary romance and romantasy)**
- A Substack essay, "Where are the middle-aged romantasy heroines?", asks why romantasy rarely features heroines over 45. It notes that readers skew older (a cited study gives an average romance reader age of 42 in 2022). The post is undated — [K.J. Aiello Substack](https://kjaiello.substack.com/p/where-are-the-middle-aged-romantasy)
- Duke's "Unsuitable" project says heroines aged 35+ are underrepresented "due to the historical precedent of Mature Romance novels being unprofitable". It adds that there are no Mature Romance imprints at major romance publishers, and that Harlequin's NEXT line was discontinued. This is older evidence that predates 2025 — [Duke Unsuitable](https://sites.duke.edu/unsuitable/age-diversity/)
- Author Gail Hart was told a heroine in her forties wouldn't sell. Lowering her to 39 was "still not good enough" (older evidence) — [Goodreads author blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/14373521-gail-hart-it-s-never-too-late?tab=book)
- A Goodreads author blog post is titled "Readers want more romances with older couples" — [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/20390072-readers-want-more-romances-with-older-couples-here-s-why-we-re-committe?tab=book). A Substack post titled "too old for happy ever after" has an active comment thread — [Maya Rodale Substack](https://mayarodale.substack.com/p/too-old-for-happy-ever-after)
- A Facebook group, "Seasoned Romance", focuses on heroes and heroines in their 30s, 40s, 50s and beyond. This came from a search summary and was not verified directly — [Delilah Devlin blog via Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/38530.Delilah_Devlin/blog?page=324)
- A Goodreads discussion asks for "historical romances where the hero is older than the heroine" — [Goodreads topic](https://www.goodreads.com/topic/mentioned_books/19449434-want-historical-romances-where-the-hero-is-older-then-the-heroine-like)

**Dark romance outside mafia**
- A July 2026 editorial says the "standard mafia dark romance" has "enormous supply". What still wins is specificity: a distinct setting, a fresh power dynamic, or a "morally grey but not cruel" hero — [TropeSmith](https://tropesmith.com/blog/romance-tropes-that-sell-2026/)
- An author blog says some readers explicitly ask for dark romance outside mafia and other familiar settings — [Athena Starr](https://athenastarr.ca/blog/dark-romance-trends-2026/)
- Trend pieces report movement toward **romance-horror** and **"femgore"** — [Atmosphere Press](https://atmospherepress.com/romantasy-trends-2026/)

**Historical romance outside Regency**
- In an industry Q&A, a Victorian-era author says "the gilded age up to the 1950s is having a bit of an upswing since the regency period has been overused". Authors also report agents rejecting books for a non-Regency setting — [Romance & Co., "Historical Romance in Peril"](https://romanceandco.substack.com/p/historical-romance-in-peril-voices)
- Fall 2026 releases span Gilded Age NYC (a Kleypas Beauty and the Beast retelling) and Ming Dynasty China — [Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/new-historical-romance-novels-fall-2026/)
- One BookTok roundup says many readers are turning to older historical romance and 2000s paranormals out of trend fatigue — [Romancing the Phone Substack](https://romancingthephone.substack.com/p/the-first-booktok-trend-roundup-of) (search summary only)

**Sports romance**
- A 2026 guide pitches "beyond hockey" M/M: basketball rivals, MMA fighters, and "FMC athletes who actually play the sport". The publisher behind the guide also publishes the recommended books — [Fractal Enigma](https://fractalenigma.com/best-mm-romance-books-2026-beyond-hockey-every-trope/)

**Disability, neurodivergence and body type**
- A March 2025 Smart Bitches "Help a Bitch Out" post (a reader column asking the community to identify a half-remembered book) features a paranormal romance with a **blind heroine and a werewolf hero**. Commenters struggled to find a match — [SBTB](https://smartbitchestrashybooks.com/2025/03/habo-mating-bond-lets-her-sense-auras/)
- Plus-size heroines have dedicated Goodreads communities: "Curvy Girls Rock!" with about 561 members, plus "Best Plus-Sized Heroine Romances" lists. This suggests established demand — [Goodreads search summary](https://www.goodreads.com/list/liked/146493576-ai-reader?page=1)

### Inferences
- Specific combinations that fit KDP/KU and rest on the above evidence:
  - **M/M orc romance**
  - **Cozy satyr or fawn romance** (low-angst monster romance)
  - **Minotaur farm or dairy romance**
  - **Sea-creature enemies-to-lovers** (and actually naming the trope in the blurb)
  - **40+ heroine romantasy**
  - **Dark romance in a non-mafia setting with a "morally grey but not cruel" hero**
  - **Gilded Age to 1950s historical romance**
  - **Female-athlete sports romance outside hockey**
- Several gaps are partly *discovery* gaps rather than supply gaps: books exist, but blurbs and keywords miss the terms readers search for (sea-creature and demon data). A new KU entrant can win just by labelling tropes precisely.

### Gaps
- No direct Reddit quotes, upvote counts or reply counts could be gathered because reddit.com was blocked. The brief's "recs for..." thread evidence still needs collecting.
- No BookTok comment, X or Threads evidence was collected.
- Romance.io tag counts were not checked.

## 2. Which requests get heavy engagement but few satisfying recommendations?

### Takeaway
No direct engagement data could be collected. The best proxies are PlotProse's classifier-based "underserved" percentages, which are vendor data and unverified, and the Monster Romance Reviews mention-versus-blurb counts.

### Cited Findings
- PlotProse's Nov 2, 2026 "Reader Demand Report" counts about 2.84M demand signals. About 39% are tagged "underserved or novel" by the TropeSmith classifier. Visible rows:

  | Trope | Signals | Tagged underserved/novel |
  |---|---|---|
  | Second Chance | 77,173 | 36% |
  | Paranormal | 63,781 | 42% |
  | Dark Romance | 44,150 | 49% |

  — [PlotProse](https://plotprose.com/romance-reader-demand-report-2026/)
- PlotProse tropes with high "underserved" shares — [Found Family](https://plotprose.com/found-family-romance-trope-demand/), [Why Choose](https://plotprose.com/why-choose-romance-trope-demand/), [Hurt/Comfort](https://plotprose.com/hurt-comfort-romance-trope-demand/):

  | Trope | Requests/signals | Tagged underserved/novel |
  |---|---|---|
  | Hurt/comfort | 1,148 | 98% (promotional page) |
  | Why Choose | 9,390 | 57% |
  | Found family | 326,665 | 45% |

- An August 2026 PlotProse page puts dark romance at about 52 units of demand per unit of published supply, and names **reverse harem** as the clearest cooling trope. These figures conflict with the November report's numbers — [PlotProse Decoded](https://plotprose.com/classroom/2026-romance-demand-report.html)
- In the sea-creature data, "enemies to lovers" gets 67 Reddit mentions against 5 blurbs — [Monster Romance Reviews](https://monsterromancereviews.substack.com/p/plenty-of-data-in-the-sea)

### Inferences
- **Hurt/comfort** and **dark romance** are the most consistently flagged as undersupplied relative to demand in this vendor data. **Paranormal** at 42% fits the separate signal that readers are returning to 2000s-style paranormal.

### Gaps
- PlotProse's methodology tags each request in isolation and has no view of what is actually on shelves. It needs independent checking.

## 3. Which trends are saturated or causing fatigue?

### Takeaway
The most-cited saturation points are: **standard mafia dark romance**; **orc romance at the top** (dominated by one series); **reverse harem / why choose** (described as cooling, though PlotProse also tags 57% of why-choose requests as underserved, which contradicts this); **same-y fae romantasy**; **hockey and sports romance**, with mixed signals; and **Regency**, as far as industry gatekeepers are concerned.

### Cited Findings
- **Mafia:** "enormous supply" — [TropeSmith](https://tropesmith.com/blog/romance-tropes-that-sell-2026/)
- **Reverse harem:** "the clearest cooling trope" — [PlotProse](https://plotprose.com/classroom/2026-romance-demand-report.html)
- **Romantasy:** an author in a 2026 roundup says audiences seem to be tiring of the same romantasy tropes. The same roundup's headline asks "Sports Romances Are Out?" — [The Everygirl](https://theeverygirl.com/book-trends-according-to-authors/) (search summary only; the page could not be fetched)
- **Tropes need a fresh angle:** readers have not gone off tropes, but a trope now needs "freshness, specificity or exceptional chemistry" — [Heptagon Books](https://www.heptagonbooks.co.uk/articles/romcom-reader-trends-2026-whats-landing)
- **Spice:** there is a reported appetite for low-spice and no-spice romance, while high heat still sells — [Heptagon Books](https://www.heptagonbooks.co.uk/articles/romance-reader-trends-2026-whats-next)
- **Contemporary:** a March 2026 post asks "Are publishers turning down contemporary romance?" — [Path2Pub](https://path2pub.wordpress.com/2026/03/02/publishing-trends-are-publishers-turning-down-contemporary-romance/)

### Inferences
- Avoid undifferentiated mafia, generic fae, and generic hockey romance.

### Gaps
- No hard sales data on fatigue.
- Sources contradict each other on whether sports romance is declining.

## 4. Which protagonist types, settings, heat levels and structures are asked for?

### Takeaway
The ages, settings, heat and character types readers ask for are:

| Dimension | What readers ask for |
|---|---|
| Age | Heroines aged 40-45+, including in romantasy |
| Historical setting | Victorian, Gilded Age to 1950s, non-Western (Ming China) |
| Heat | Low/no-spice that still feels very romantic; also higher-heat non-Regency historicals |
| Structure | Low-angst ("cozy") monster romance |
| Disability and body type | Disabled heroines (blind heroine + paranormal hero), plus-size heroines |
| Sports | Female athletes who compete |
| Hero type | Morally grey but not cruel |

### Cited Findings
- **Older heroines:** see section 1 — [Aiello](https://kjaiello.substack.com/p/where-are-the-middle-aged-romantasy), [Duke](https://sites.duke.edu/unsuitable/age-diversity/)
- **Non-Regency historicals** "tend to run hotter on average" — [Bookdot spice guide](https://bookdot.app/blog/historical-romance-spice-level-guide/). A commercial aggregator lists emerging niches in **Victorian mysteries** and **clean Western frontier romance** (low reliability) — [Accio](https://www.accio.com/business/top-selling-historical-romance-novels)
- **Neurodivergence and chronic illness:** commentary credits *The Kiss Quotient* and Talia Hibbert's Brown Sisters trilogy with readers who had "never seen that experience centred in a steamy, joyful romance" — [Author Ever After](https://www.authoreverafter.com/romance-market-trends-reader-expectations/)
- **Diversity:** a self-run survey of 1,000+ romance readers (updated Feb 2026) lists "Diversity (48%)" among its results. The question's framing is unclear — [Guilty Chapters](https://www.guiltychapters.com/romance-reading-statistics-survey-results/)
- **Sapphic readers:** a sapphic fiction reader survey drew 2,825 respondents, with older-skewing demographics — [Jae Fiction](https://jae-fiction.com/sapphic-fiction-reader-survey-part-one/)

### Gaps
- Nothing found on demand for specific occupations or cultures beyond the examples above, apart from one historical release set in Ming China.
- Medieval romance demand was not covered by any source found.

## 5. Amazon/KU evidence on supply

### Takeaway
Only weak supply evidence was collected. The monster romance reports cite Amazon search and bestseller composition. No category bestseller ranks, review counts or result counts were pulled directly, because amazon.com fetching was not attempted after other hosts were blocked.

### Cited Findings
- **Orc romance:** five of the top dozen orc results are Finley Fenn's Orc Sworn. There was no M/M orc romance in the M/M top 25 (Oct 20, 2026) — [Monster Romance Reviews](https://monsterromancereviews.substack.com/p/horde-mentality-whos-filling-the)
- **Demon romance:** 67 bestselling Amazon authors, of whom only 4 appear in social discussion — [Monster Romance Reviews](https://monsterromancereviews.substack.com/p/the-devil-is-in-the-data)
- **KU sports romance outside hockey:** KU-eligible football and baseball titles exist. Examples are *Saint: A Football Romance*, *The Forever Play*, and the Denver Bandits baseball series — [Amazon KU sports romance](https://www.amazon.com/Sports-Romance-Kindle-Unlimited-Eligible/s?rh=n%3A10387327011%2Cp_n_feature_twenty_browse-bin%3A13054657011), [Amazon football KU search](https://www.amazon.com/football-romance-kindle-unlimited/s?k=football+romance+kindle+unlimited)
- **Mature romance:** no Mature Romance imprint exists at the big romance publishers (older evidence). This leaves the self-published / KU route open — [Duke](https://sites.duke.edu/unsuitable/age-diversity/)

### Inferences
- In self-publishing terms, M/M orc romance and 40+ heroine romantasy show the clearest "no incumbent" signal. Minotaur has a dominant reference title (*Morning Glory Milking Farm*), so it is proven demand with some competition.

### Gaps
- No Amazon BSR, review-count or Publisher Rocket-style competition data was collected.
- Each candidate niche needs a manual Amazon or KU check: count KU results for exact keyword phrases, and look at the bestseller ranks (BSR) of the top 10 results.
