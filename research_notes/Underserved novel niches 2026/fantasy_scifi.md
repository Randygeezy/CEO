## Q1. Which SFF subgenres and premise combinations do readers repeatedly request in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
Trade and genre-blog commentary from 2026 consistently points to **cozy fantasy** and **cozy/crafting/base-building LitRPG** as the niches where demand most outruns supply. Hybrid **LitRPG romantasy** is described as an emerging niche. I could not retrieve the actual reader request threads ("looking for…", "I wish there were more…") that would prove this at the reader level.

### Cited Findings
- A K-lytics-based keyword analysis reportedly ranks "cozy fantasy" (45.9) above "epic fantasy" (41.4). The same piece argues demand outruns supply in the "Cozy, LitRPG, and YA Dark Fantasy" gaps. — [Author Media, "Zeitgeist: Cozy Fantasy and LitRPG SURGE as Hottest Niches in 2026"](https://www.authormedia.com/zeitgeist-cozy-fantasy-and-litrpg-surge-as-hottest-niches-in-2026/) (search snippet only)
- K-lytics' 2026 genre report reportedly names cozy fantasy its top opportunity: a 23.1 sales-to-competition ratio, about 60 daily sales per top-20 title, and 2,576 competing books. It also reportedly finds that 78 of the top 100 Fantasy titles are in KU and take 83% of that list's royalties. — [Author Media](https://www.authormedia.com/zeitgeist-cozy-fantasy-and-litrpg-surge-as-hottest-niches-in-2026/) (secondary source reporting K-lytics, search snippet only)
- A trade outlet describes "a nascent hybrid niche—LitRPG Romantasy—blending progression mechanics with romantic plot arcs," alongside growth in cozy fantasy and space opera. — [Good e-Reader, "Romantasy, and LitRPG propelled Science Fiction & Fantasy to Record Sales"](https://goodereader.com/blog/digital-publishing/romantasy-and-litrpg-propelled-science-fiction-fantasy-to-record-sales) (search snippet)
- A 2026 genre guide reportedly calls base-building and crafting the fastest-growing lane in 2026. It lists what readers ask for as "dungeon core, system apocalypse, crunchy stats, cozy crafting, base-building, or tower climbing." — [State of LitRPG 2026 (litrpgtools.com)](https://www.litrpgtools.com/guides/state-of-litrpg-2026) and [ANVIL N INK LitRPG guide 2026](https://anvilnink.com/litrpg-books/) (search snippets; the summary did not say which claim came from which source)
- A 2026 retrospective describes a segmented market: "readers who strictly read 'Crunchy Town-Builders' and readers who only touch 'Progression Fantasy with light LitRPG elements.'" — [LitRPG Reads, "LitRPG Novels in 2026 vs LitRPG Books in 2016"](https://litrpgreads.com/blog/litrpg/litrpg-novels-2026-vs-litrpg-books-in-2016-what-a-decade-changed) (search snippet)
- Progression fantasy without visible game mechanics reportedly makes up about 38% of top-rated books in combined LitRPG/progression reader communities. The figure comes from LitRPGTools' own community data and is not independently verified. — [State of LitRPG 2026](https://www.litrpgtools.com/guides/state-of-litrpg-2026) (search snippet)
- SF: K-lytics Summer 2026 reportedly maps 183 SF&F subcategories, with "Science Fiction-Adventure: up 49%" and "Science Fiction-Colonization: down 96%," the worst performer. — [Conquest Publishing, "The 2026 Genre Trend Series: Day 2"](https://conquestpublishing.substack.com/p/the-2026-genre-trend-series-day-2) (search snippet; verify against the K-lytics report)
- SF: Commentary says 2026 SFF growth sits in tightly defined subgenres such as climate fiction, Indigenous futurism and "slow sci-fi," with a thematic shift toward optimism and community. — [IngramSpark blog](https://www.ingramspark.com/blog/why-sci-fi-fantasy-are-booming-and-what-indie-authors-need-to-know-to-make-the-most-of-it) and [Shadow Ghostwriter 2026 guide](https://shadowghostwriter.com/writing-science-fiction-in-2026-guide-to-crafting-worlds-that-sell) (search snippets; marketing blogs, low reliability)
- Solarpunk/hopepunk: A 2026 Substack essay ("We've had enough dystopian fiction") says solarpunk is growing. A May 2026 piece argues readers are drawn to hopeful SF. Neither gives sales data. — [Colette Sevierberg Substack](https://colettesevierberg.substack.com/p/weve-had-enough-dystopian-fiction); [Grattan Street Press, 2026-05-18](https://grattanstreetpress.com/2026/05/18/solarpunk-is-this-science-fiction-genre-inspiring-hope-or-delusion/)

### Inferences
- The most consistent cross-source signal is **cozy + progression/crafting**: a low-stakes slice-of-life setting, a crafting or building loop and visible progression. Sub-niches inside this space (specific crafts, specific settings) are probably where an indie gap remains, because the broad label is now heavily used. See the Q5 supply evidence.
- "Soft" progression (no stat screens) as a distinct audience segment suggests positioning such as "progression fantasy, no system" can be a differentiator.
- In SF, "adventure" rising while "colonization" collapses (if accurate) points toward adventure-forward space opera and away from colony-building SF.

### Gaps
- No verified Reddit or Royal Road request threads with upvote and reply counts. Both domains were blocked. This is the brief's core evidence type and it is missing.
- No r/Fantasy 2025/2026 survey results were found. A ListChallenges "R/Fantasy Top Books of 2025" page surfaced, but it looks undated and stale: [listchallenges](https://www.listchallenges.com/rfantasy-top-books-of-2025).

## Q2. Which requests get attention but few good recommendations (unmet demand)?

### Takeaway
The only indirect evidence found concerns **older/middle-aged protagonists** and **family-centered LitRPG**. In both, recommenders fall back on a small set of older titles, which suggests thin supply. This is weak, anecdotal evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **Middle-aged MCs (general fantasy).** A Goodreads Fantasy Buddy Reads thread, "Books with middle-aged MCs," exists. Its recommendations reportedly lean on older backlist titles (Jane Lindskold's *Child of a Rainless Year*, with a protagonist turning 51; Carol Berg's catalog of protagonists in their 30s and up). — [Goodreads, Fantasy Buddy Reads: "Books with middle-aged MCs"](https://www.goodreads.com/topic/show/21820795-books-with-middle-aged-mcs) (thread date not confirmed)
- **Older MCs, hopeful tone.** Another Goodreads recommendation thread points to *Kings of the Wyld* because the main characters are older men and the story is hopeful. — [Goodreads, SciFi and Fantasy Book Club: whimsy/tension/adventure request](https://www.goodreads.com/topic/show/22647041-seek-non-ya-fantasy-novel-with-a-balance-of-whimsy-tension-and-adventu?tab=book)
- **Retired-adventurer cozy.** *Slayers of Old* (2025), about retired magic users running a bookshop, was described in a quoted review as "a delightful, somewhat cozy fantasy with a fast pace and snappy supporting characters." It is one of the few close fits for an older-MC cozy book. — [Wikipedia: Slayers of Old](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slayers_of_Old)
- **Family-centered LitRPG (dad or parent MC).** In a series where a whole family goes through a portal into a game world, the father is reportedly 40. Reviewers call the whole-family angle unusual for LitRPG but complain about abrupt POV shifts away from the family. — [Goodreads book 52991660](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/52991660); [Goodreads book 58513099](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/58513099)
- **Non-weapon / less violent fantasy.** An author blog post titled "Fantasy that isn't heavy on the weapons?" reflects readers asking for lower-violence fantasy. — [Goodreads author blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/15524835-fantasy-that-isn-t-heavy-on-the-weapons) (date unknown, possibly older)
- **Competent heroine, light romance space opera.** On a book-recommendation thread on Ilona Andrews' blog, a commenter suggested Tanya Huff's *Valor* series for "kick-ass competent heroine in space opera, with little romance, lots of action." — [Ilona Andrews blog: TKWNKM support group recs](https://ilona-andrews.com/blog/book-recommendations-thread-the-tkwnkm-support-group/) (date unconfirmed; recommending a backlist series from the 2000s suggests few newer options)

### Inferences
- **Candidate niche: an older (40s–60s) protagonist in cozy or progression fantasy**, such as a retired adventurer, a parent or a second-career MC. Recommenders keep reaching for the same handful of traditionally published or backlist titles, which suggests an indie KU gap. Treat this as a hypothesis until it is checked against r/ProgressionFantasy and r/cozyfantasy request threads.
- **Candidate niche: family-unit LitRPG** (parent MC protecting or progressing with children). It is described as unusual, and the reader complaint about POV dilution gives a concrete craft brief: keep the POV on the family.
- **Candidate niche: female-led military or competent-heroine space opera with little romance.** Recommendations default to older series.

### Gaps
- No upvote or reply counts and no "nothing like this exists" comments could be retrieved.
- No evidence was found either way on specific-mythology requests (for example West African, Slavic, Mesoamerican or Southeast Asian cultivation and fantasy), non-Chinese cultivation, academy-free progression, or non-combat MCs in progression. These were planned queries that ran out of budget or returned nothing usable.

## Q3. What are readers tired of (saturated trends)?

### Takeaway
Royal Road listings suggest **harem** is something readers actively avoid: authors advertise its absence as a selling point. **Reincarnation** and **system apocalypse** look extremely common, which signals saturation but not proven fatigue. Colonization SF reportedly crashed in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Trending and weekly-popular Royal Road blurbs advertise absence as a feature, for example "What not to expect: Harem or Smut," with "No Harem" or "Minimal Romance." — [Royal Road Trending](https://www.royalroad.com/fictions/trending); [Royal Road Popular this week](https://www.royalroad.com/fictions/weekly-popular) (search snippet; undated snapshot)
- Royal Road renamed its "Harem" tag "Multiple Lovers." — [Royal Road knowledge base](https://www.royalroad.com/support/knowledgebase/98)
- In a Royal Road forum thread, a reader says they filter out harem and reincarnation tags with advanced search. Commenters also note that wrong tags attract readers who leave low ratings. — [Royal Road forum: "Tags & Content Warnings"](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/165229) (date unconfirmed)
- Reincarnation appears across most "Popular this week" and "Trending" listings, often paired with LitRPG, progression and isekai tags. — [Royal Road weekly popular](https://www.royalroad.com/fictions/weekly-popular?page=23) (search summary)
- System apocalypse is crowded. Tao Wong's System Apocalypse universe alone has several spin-offs (for example the *Relentless* line, three or more books), and other authors reuse the premise (*Apocalypse Tamer*, *Systems of the Apocalypse*). At least one title markets itself as a satire of the typical LitRPG apocalypse. — [MIT Press Bookstore listing, A Fist Full of Credits](https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9781990491092); [AbeBooks, Redeemer of the Dead](https://www.abebooks.com/9781775058748/Redeemer-Dead-LitRPG-Apocalypse-System-1775058743/plp)
- "Science Fiction-Colonization: down 96%" in K-lytics Summer 2026. — [Conquest Publishing Substack](https://conquestpublishing.substack.com/p/the-2026-genre-trend-series-day-2) (search snippet)
- Release cadence: "if a series isn't finished or doesn't have a rapid release schedule (a book every 1-2 months), many readers won't start it." — [State of LitRPG 2026](https://www.litrpgtools.com/guides/state-of-litrpg-2026) (search snippet)
- Dystopia fatigue: the essay title "We've had enough dystopian fiction" (2026). — [Substack](https://colettesevierberg.substack.com/p/weve-had-enough-dystopian-fiction)

### Inferences
- Avoid or de-emphasize: harem, generic reincarnation/isekai openings, generic system apocalypse, colony-building SF and grimdark dystopia. Saying "no harem" outright in the blurb looks like an established positioning tactic.
- Rapid release (every 1–2 months) is a market expectation in LitRPG/KU and constrains which niches a single author can realistically serve.

### Gaps
- No quantified fatigue data (polls or survey percentages). The Royal Road snapshots are undated.

## Q4. Specific protagonist types, magic systems, settings, or structures requested

### Takeaway
The structures with the most commentary support are **crafting, base/town-building, dungeon core and tower climbing**, plus **soft or no-system progression**. For protagonist types, the evidence is suggestive but thin for older MCs, parent/family MCs and female MCs in crafting or cozy progression.

### Cited Findings
- Structures readers reportedly seek: "dungeon core, system apocalypse, crunchy stats, cozy crafting, base-building, or tower climbing." — [litrpgtools State of LitRPG 2026](https://www.litrpgtools.com/guides/state-of-litrpg-2026) / [ANVIL N INK](https://anvilnink.com/litrpg-books/) (search snippets)
- A "Crunchy Town-Builders" reader segment. — [LitRPG Reads 2026](https://litrpgreads.com/blog/litrpg/litrpg-novels-2026-vs-litrpg-books-in-2016-what-a-decade-changed)
- Female MCs: A Royal Road forum thread argues male MCs sell better in LitRPG/progression because the audience is mostly male and self-selects. It names cozy fantasy, crafting LitRPG and survival progression as subgenres more open to female MCs, and cites *Azarinth Healer* as a female-led success. It advises that the blurb, cover and tags must signal clearly who the book is for. **About 650 days old (early 2025 or before), so this is older evidence.** — [Royal Road forum: "Female vs Male MC from a Business Perspective"](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/148012?page=1)
- A slow-burn, character-driven progression fantasy about "second chances, family, mystery, and power earned one step at a time" appears in 2026 reader-guide listings. — [LitRPG Critic: new releases, 2026-07-03](https://litrpgcritic.com/blog/2026-07-03-new-litrpg-progression-fantasy-releases-worth-reading-right-now/) (search snippet)
- Cozy fantasy is defined around hope, kindness, community and found family, with romance optional ("healthy romance"). — [Wikipedia: Cozy fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cozy_fantasy)

### Inferences
- A combination worth testing: **female or older MC + crafting/town-building + cozy tone + light or no romance + explicit "no harem."** Each part has some support. I found no evidence that the full combination is saturated.
- Family and second-chance themes inside progression look like a lightly served emotional register.

### Gaps
- No usable evidence on requested magic systems (rune or ritual crafting, music, cooking), specific mythologies, non-combat MCs (healer, merchant, farmer) in progression, portal fantasy variants, or post-apocalyptic and military SF reader requests.

## Q5. Amazon / KU / Royal Road supply evidence

### Takeaway
Cozy crafting and slice-of-life LitRPG titles already rank on Amazon's Cozy Fantasy bestseller list and are mostly in KU. So the broad "cozy crafting LitRPG" label has real demand and growing supply. Gaps are more likely in narrower sub-niches. A K-lytics competition figure (2,576 competing cozy fantasy books against strong per-title sales) supports favorable demand-to-supply economics.

### Cited Findings
- Amazon "Best Cozy Fantasy Fiction" list (undated snapshot): *The Builder's Path (Book 1): A Crafting LitRPG Progression Fantasy* at #8; *Infinite Forge: OP MC Cozy Crafting Slice of Life Litrpg* at #11. — [Amazon Best Sellers: Cozy Fantasy Fiction](https://www.amazon.com/Best-Sellers-Kindle-Store-Cozy-Fantasy-Fiction/zgbs/digital-text/214873986011)
- Many cozy crafting LitRPG titles carry KU availability; Amazon search pages show titles such as *A Cozy Crafting LitRPG of Smithing, Small-Town Life*. — [Amazon search: cozy fantasy litrpg](https://www.amazon.com/cozy-fantasy-litrpg/s?k=cozy+fantasy+litrpg); [Amazon search: litrpg crafting](https://www.amazon.com/litrpg-crafting/s?k=litrpg+crafting)
- A cozy slice-of-life LitRPG reportedly ranked #91 in a K-lytics Fantasy top-100 snapshot. — [Author Media](https://www.authormedia.com/zeitgeist-cozy-fantasy-and-litrpg-surge-as-hottest-niches-in-2026/) (search snippet)
- Cozy fantasy: 23.1 sales-to-competition ratio, about 60 daily sales per top-20 title, 2,576 competing books (K-lytics via Author Media, as in Q1). — [Author Media](https://www.authormedia.com/zeitgeist-cozy-fantasy-and-litrpg-surge-as-hottest-niches-in-2026/)
- **Platform flag:** One search summary cited a Wikipedia snippet saying Amazon rebranded Kindle Unlimited as "Kindle+" in October 2026. This is a single unverified mention and should be confirmed because it affects KU strategy. — [Wikipedia: Kindle Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindle_Store) (unverified)

### Inferences
- "Cozy crafting LitRPG" as a broad label is **no longer an empty niche**: titles with keyword-stuffed subtitles rank in the top 10–20 of the category. Differentiation probably has to come from protagonist type (older, parent, female), the specific craft or setting, or SF framing (cozy SF crafting is barely visible in the results).
- High KU penetration (78 of the top 100 Fantasy titles) supports KU as the default channel for these niches.

### Gaps
- No Royal Road follower or rating counts for any niche, because the site was unreachable.
- No BSR or review-count analysis of specific candidate niches (older-MC cozy, family LitRPG, female-led crafting, solarpunk, military SF). This needs live Amazon category checks.
- No current data on space opera, military SF, post-apocalyptic or portal-fantasy competition. The K-lytics subcategory figures were only partly visible through a snippet.
